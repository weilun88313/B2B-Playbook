# Case study

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-30

A page titled “How Acme achieved transformation” with a 40% efficiency quote and no date is a brochure. [Content strategy](content-strategy.md) decides whether a proof URL belongs on the map. This page owns **how a named customer’s context, action, and result become something a champion can forward**—and when you must refuse to publish.

[Positioning](../02-product-marketing/positioning.md) names the alternative and the result. The case study is **one dated instance** of that result, with the friction left in. It is not a white paper, not a testimonial wall, and not a logo with a sentence.

## Use this when

- A customer will go on the record, and sales already tells their story in rooms.
- Evaluation threads ask “who like us has done this?” and the answer is a deck slide.
- Marketing wants twelve case studies and you have two customers who would survive a callback.

## Do not use this when

- Nobody will put their name on it. An anonymous “leading enterprise” is not this page.
- The decision-page map still lacks category, alternative, or comparison. Write those first.
- You need the claim standard for every sentence on the site. That is still planned as proof-and-claims.
- The story is a launch announcement. That is a date, not a case.

## Words you will use

| Word | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **Named** | Company (or a seat + company the reader can check) that agreed to be public |
| **Alternative** | What they were doing before—not “manual processes” |
| **Action** | What they changed in operations, not what your product can do |
| **Result** | A number or risk they already owned, with a date and a denominator |
| **Friction left in** | What was hard, what they would not do again, where you were not the reason |

## One rule

**If a skeptical champion cannot verify the before, the change, and the result, do not ship.** A quote without a situation is a testimonial. A metric without a date or a base is a slogan. Invented or composite customers are an incident.

## Operating method

### Step 1: earn the right to name them

Write: *&#123;company&#125; will let us say &#123;these three facts&#125; because &#123;who approved, on what date&#125;.* If legal or the customer will only allow a logo, you have a logo, not a case study. Do not “anonymize” your way around a no.

Prefer the customer whose **alternative matches live deals**, not the logo that photographs well.

### Step 2: steal the structure from the deal, not from a template vendor

One story, one job:

1. **Who and the job** — seat, operating context, why they are in the ICP
2. **Before** — the alternative in their words
3. **Trigger** — what made staying expensive
4. **Action** — what they actually changed (owners, what was replaced)
5. **Result** — dated, checkable, in a unit they already report
6. **Friction** — implementation cost, what stayed, where the alternative was better
7. **Forward line** — one sentence a champion can paste upstairs
8. **Next step** — usually the [comparison](../07-website-and-conversion/comparison-page.md) or a scoped [demo request](../07-website-and-conversion/demo-request.md), not “read more stories”

If you cannot write the friction paragraph, you do not understand the deal yet—or you are hiding it.

### Step 3: label every number

Each figure: source (customer, your system, third party), date, and what it is *of*. “40% faster” needs faster than what, measured how, over which window. If the customer will not let you show the math, drop the number. A true qualitative result beats a fake percentage.

### Step 4: write for the next champion, not for the customer who already bought

The reader is in an evaluation thread. They need “people like us left &#123;alternative&#125; when &#123;trigger&#125; and got &#123;result&#125;.” They do not need your product tour, a CEO letter, or five persona tabs. One URL, one job—same rule as [content strategy](content-strategy.md).

### Step 5: put it where sales will actually send it

Attach the URL to the decision-page map and to the [homepage](../07-website-and-conversion/homepage.md) proof block only if the story matches the scan. A case study that sales rewrites every time is unpublished.

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] Written approval to use the name and the facts.
- [ ] Alternative and trigger would be recognized in current deals.
- [ ] Every number has a date, a base, and a source—or the number is gone.
- [ ] Friction is on the page.
- [ ] No composite, no “Fortune 500 manufacturer,” no invented quote.
- [ ] Sales will paste the URL without a rewrite.
- [ ] Category / alternative / comparison pages exist or this story is not the first URL you write.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Sales forwards | Times the URL is pasted into an evaluation thread |
| Champion reuse | Times the *customer’s* forward line appears without seller edits |
| Approval lag | Stories killed because we wrote before we had permission |
| Wrong-logo traffic | Readers from a segment you will not sell (retire or noindex) |

Do not treat case-study pageviews, “content downloads,” or count of published stories as the program.

## Common mistakes

- Publishing before approval, then redacting into mush.
- Efficiency percentages with no before.
- Making the customer a backdrop for a feature list.
- Twelve thin stories instead of two that sales uses.
- Leading the content map with proof while comparison is still empty.

## Sources and evidence boundary

This is an owner-maintained operating synthesis.

- Dated, checkable proof and “include where it was hard” follow the comparison/proof discipline already on [positioning](../02-product-marketing/positioning.md) (April Dunford’s public work).
- No invented names: already a [content strategy](content-strategy.md) pre-flight rule.
- “Named customer, operational before/after, metric the buyer already owns” is standard operator practice (Foundation-style narrative, Exit Five teardowns). Those firms’ paid templates are not reproduced. This page is not legal advice on publicity rights.

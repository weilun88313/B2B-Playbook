**Last reviewed:** 2026-09-02

A biography on `/about` and a founder who pastes the blog into LinkedIn are both “founder content.” Neither is a story. This page is **which lived experience makes the founder’s point of view relevant**—so a buyer can repeat it, and so [LinkedIn organic](../04-channels-and-distribution/linkedin-organic.md) has something to say that is not a feature announcement.

[Messaging](../02-product-marketing/messaging.md) owns the product hierarchy. [Content strategy](content-strategy.md) owns which questions get a URL. The founder story is one **perception** the person can actually tell. If they will not tell it, it is not a [marketing advantage](../04-channels-and-distribution/channel-strategy.md).

This is not an origin-myth workshop, not a ghostwritten “journey,” and not thought leadership (still planned). Thought leadership is an original idea the market did not have. A founder story is a life the founder did have.

## Use this when

- The company wants “founder-led” as a calendar of product posts.
- The `/about` page is a résumé and a stock photo.
- Perception work named a story only the founder can prove, and nobody wrote it down.
- Outsiders already know the person; the product story still does not use that fact.

## Do not use this when

- Positioning and the primary ICP are empty. Finish [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md) and [positioning](../02-product-marketing/positioning.md) first.
- The founder will not appear, will not write, and will not sit for interviews. Do not invent a personal brand they will not staff.
- You need the feed mechanics. That is [LinkedIn organic](../04-channels-and-distribution/linkedin-organic.md).
- You need a named customer’s result. That is a [case study](case-study.md).

## Words you will use

| Word | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **Lived scene** | A specific job, failure, or constraint the founder actually had—not “passionate about the space” |
| **Perception they own** | The one storyline only this person can make believable |
| **Advantage** | Founder distribution or founder/market fit *if* they will tell the story. Wish is not an advantage |
| **Surface** | About page, first-meeting opener, a repeating organic idea—not twelve employee advocates |

## One rule

**If it is still true with the product name removed, it can run. If only the founder could have lived it, it belongs here.** A feature list in first person is an ad. A childhood-to-Series-B reel with no buying job is theater.

## Operating method

### Step 1: start from a perception, not from a timeline

Take the three or four perceptions already on [content strategy](content-strategy.md). Circle **one** the founder can prove from a scene they were in. Write it in the buyer’s mouth (“Shared inboxes break when someone is paid on queue health”), not “we were founded in 2019.”

If none of the perceptions need the founder, do not force a personal brand. Ship product pages and a [case study](case-study.md) instead.

### Step 2: write the scene before the bio

One paragraph:

- the job they had (seat and constraint);
- what broke;
- what they now refuse to sell as the fix;
- why that still matches the [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md).

Cut: school, every employer, “we assembled a world-class team,” and any claim a customer cannot check. A second founder gets a scene only if they own a different perception. Two people telling the same origin is a duplicate.

### Step 3: decide whether this is an advantage

It is an advantage only if **both** are true: the person already has relationships or a room in this market, **and** they will show up on a cadence you can staff. If they hate writing, hire the motion they will actually do (talks, customer dinners, a monthly interview)—or drop the advantage. Do not appoint twelve “employee advocates” to cover a founder who will not speak. That failure mode is already on [LinkedIn organic](../04-channels-and-distribution/linkedin-organic.md).

### Step 4: pick two surfaces, not a content engine

Default:

- **About / team:** the scene, not the résumé. The product story still lives on the [homepage](../07-website-and-conversion/homepage.md).
- **Repeating idea:** the perception, in the founder’s voice, on the channel they will actually use.

A campaign still needs a [GACCS](../../templates/campaign-brief.md). The Goal is the perception, not “personal brand lift.”

### Step 5: refuse the costume

Do not ghostwrite a voice the person will not say on a call. Do not claim founder/market fit because the TAM slide says so. Do not use the founder story to paper over empty [messaging](../02-product-marketing/messaging.md).

## Teaching fill (invented—not a customer)

Founder ran customer ops. Alternative: shared inbox.

| Field | Fill |
|---|---|
| Perception | “Shared inbox breaks when someone is paid on queue health.” |
| Scene | They owned a 12-person queue; SLA lived in a spreadsheet; a missed enterprise pack cost a renewal. They will not sell an ITSM rollout to that team. |
| Advantage? | Yes if they already post in ops rooms and will keep a weekly scene. No if marketing must invent the posts. |
| Surfaces | About page = the scene. Organic = the perception. Homepage still names the product. |
| Will not do | Childhood reel. “We’re excited to announce.” Twelve employee advocates. |

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] Positioning and a primary ICP exist.
- [ ] The story ladders to one named perception, not a timeline.
- [ ] The scene is specific enough to survive without the product name.
- [ ] We did not call it an advantage unless they have the room and a cadence.
- [ ] About page is not a résumé dump.
- [ ] Homepage still passes its own scan; this story does not replace the door.
- [ ] LinkedIn (or the chosen channel) has a named speaker and a stop rule.
- [ ] No ghostwritten voice they will not say on a call.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Repeat | Times a buyer or seller retells the scene without the slide |
| HDYHAU / sales mention | Humans who name the person, not only the logo |
| Cadence kept | Weeks the founder actually showed up vs the plan |
| Advantage honesty | Whether we still claim founder distribution after they went quiet |

Do not count followers, “thought leadership” posts, or a published `/about` as the outcome.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a résumé as a story.
- Forcing a personal brand on a founder who will not staff it.
- Using the founder as a feature announcer.
- Writing one origin for every cofounder.
- Letting `/about` replace the [homepage](../07-website-and-conversion/homepage.md) scan.
- Calling it thought leadership when it is only autobiography.
- Measuring impressions instead of whether the perception moved.

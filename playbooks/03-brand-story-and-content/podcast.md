A podcast is a repeatable listening experience, not a series of sales calls with recording enabled. It works when a defined audience values the conversations enough to return and the team can sustain research, hosting and editing.

## Decide whether a series is justified

Use this format when the subject benefits from nuance, recurring voices or access to practitioner experience. If the idea mainly requires a diagram or a short answer, use video or writing. Define a limited first season so the team can learn before committing indefinitely.

## Produce a useful episode

1. Write the audience promise and the editorial angle. Specify what makes this conversation different from a guest's standard promotional appearance.
2. Research the guest's actual work. Agree on a useful decision or incident to explore. Send topic areas and recording expectations without forcing every answer into a script.
3. Confirm permissions. Cover recording, editing, publication, clips and any confidential boundaries. Do not imply that appearing on the show endorses the product.
4. Record a progression. Establish the situation, choice, alternatives, result and what the guest would change. Ask for concrete examples and distinguish recollection from documented results.
5. Edit for clarity without changing meaning. Remove avoidable repetition, check factual statements and correct introductions. Create a transcript or accessible text alternative, show notes and source links.
6. Distribute the episode where the intended audience already listens or reads. Offer guests ready-to-use material without making promotion a hidden condition of participation.

## Worked example

A fictional company pilots four conversations about failed systems migrations. Each guest explains one decision, the warning signs and a repair. The host does not force a product mention into every episode.

Listeners ask for a checklist that compares the incidents. The team publishes an original synthesis with permission and links to full episodes. A guest later becomes a commercial contact, but that does not retroactively turn the interview into a sales meeting or prove podcast ROI.

## Episode brief

```text
Audience and recurring promise:
Guest's relevant experience:
Specific incident or decision:
Questions that reveal alternatives and tradeoffs:
Claims to fact-check:
Recording, editing and clip permissions:
Accessible transcript and show notes:
Distribution owner:
What this episode teaches us about the series:
```

## Evaluate a season

Review relevant repeat listening, substantive feedback, production cost and guest experience. Platform metrics have different definitions, so avoid adding incompatible counts into one “audience” number. Ask whether the team has enough original questions for another season. A respectful pause is better than filling the feed with conversations that no longer fulfill the promise.

A webinar earns its place when live explanation or participation helps the audience do something they could not get as easily from a short article. Registration is an expression of interest in the session, not automatic permission for every subsequent sales action.

## Design around a useful outcome

Use a webinar for a walkthrough, discussion, critique or workshop that benefits from questions. Write what an attendee should understand or be able to do afterward. Choose a recording or written guide if live attendance adds little.

## Prepare, run and follow through

1. Pick one audience question and a concrete outcome. Recruit speakers for relevant experience rather than audience size alone. Agree on the product's role and disclose sponsorship or relationships where appropriate.
2. Build a run of show with timing, host, speaker transitions, interaction and backup. Rehearse audio, screen sharing and any demo. Keep a recorded fallback for a fragile live environment.
3. Make the registration promise accurate. State duration, audience, format, recording availability and how submitted details will be used. Ask only for context that has a real purpose.
4. Prepare accessibility and moderation. Arrange captions where supported, readable slides, a clear question channel and a plan for abusive or sensitive questions. Tell participants when recording starts.
5. Deliver the promised task before the offer. Use an example attendees can follow. Keep questions you cannot answer in a follow-up list rather than improvising unsupported advice.
6. Send the promised resources and route responses by what people requested. A demo request, a substantive question and an absent registrant should not receive identical follow-up.

## Worked example

A fictional operations team hosts a 40-minute workshop on designing a lead-routing fallback. The session uses a blank routing map, walks through an absent-owner scenario and lets attendees identify their own unowned queue.

The host shares the worksheet and recording afterward. Two attendees request product evaluations; others ask implementation questions. Sales receives only the relevant requests with context. Attendance alone is not recorded as a qualified opportunity.

## Run-of-show sheet

| Moment | Owner and required preparation |
|---|---|
| Before broadcast | Audio, demo, permissions, captions and backup |
| Opening | Promise, participation rules and recording notice |
| Main task | Example, transitions and planned interaction |
| Questions | Moderator, answer boundaries and follow-up log |
| Close | Useful resource and optional next action |
| Afterward | Delivery, response routing and content permissions |

## Review the session

Separate registrations, attendance, meaningful participation and requested next steps. Compare production effort with the value of the resulting learning and reusable material. Investigate where people leave, but do not assume every departure means poor content. Avoid repeating a webinar solely because the registration count looked large.

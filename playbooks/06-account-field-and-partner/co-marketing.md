**Published:** 2026-09-12 · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

Co-marketing is a shared campaign or asset with a defined audience benefit and a finite delivery plan. It can be one activity within a broader partnership. Make the value, workload, and follow-up agreement concrete before production begins.

## Find an activity worth combining

Choose a subject where each partner contributes something useful: expertise, evidence, a complementary demonstration, or trusted access to the right audience. Two logos on a generic webinar do not create a joint point of view.

## Write the joint brief

1. **Define the audience question.** Agree who the activity serves and what they will learn or accomplish. Keep the topic narrow enough for both partners to contribute substantively.
2. **Allocate the work.** Assign research, writing, design, delivery, promotion, approvals, and customer support. Give each deliverable one accountable owner and a deadline.
3. **Agree promotion precisely.** Specify placements and dates rather than promising “social support.” Compare audience relevance and expected reach honestly; do not guarantee results neither party controls.
4. **Set registration and data rules.** Explain to participants which organizations receive which information and for what purpose. Separate event delivery from optional marketing permissions as applicable. Avoid automatic list swaps.
5. **Rehearse delivery and follow-up.** Test links, forms, speaker transitions, resource delivery, and the route for explicit consultation requests. Agree who answers questions that span both products.
6. **Close the campaign together.** Reconcile spend, contributions, participation, useful feedback, and accepted follow-up. Review what each partner received before committing to another activity.

## Worked example

A fictional security consultant and access-management vendor run a workshop on offboarding. The consultant teaches process ownership; the vendor demonstrates one implementation. Registration explains the hosts and follow-up options.

Participants receive the promised checklist. Only those requesting a consultation enter that workflow under the agreed data rules. The partners compare attendance and completed exercises as educational outcomes, then review consultation progression separately.

## Resolve imbalance early

If one partner cannot deliver an agreed promotion, change the plan before launch rather than hiding the shortfall. If the campaign attracts the wrong audience, inspect the promise and placement. A campaign can be well executed yet poorly selected; the review should distinguish those failures.

**Published:** 2026-09-12 · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

Direct mail uses a physical item to support a relevant business conversation. Its value comes from usefulness and context, not the recipient's sense of obligation. Begin with a reason the item belongs in the workflow.

## Decide whether a physical item helps

Consider mail when an existing relationship or appropriate business context makes delivery welcome and a physical format adds value. A working booklet for a planned workshop may help; an expensive surprise gift may create inconvenience or policy problems.

## Prepare a controlled send

1. **Define the recipient benefit.** Explain what the item helps the person do and why a digital version is insufficient. If there is no clear answer, use the simpler route.
2. **Check eligibility and gift rules.** Review applicable marketing, privacy, procurement, and employer policies. Public-sector and regulated recipients may require additional review. Do not use gifts to influence a restricted decision.
3. **Confirm a suitable address.** Prefer a recipient-approved business delivery route. Do not infer or purchase a home address to bypass remote-work uncertainty. Offer a digital alternative or an easy decline.
4. **Keep the package practical.** Make sender identity and purpose clear. Avoid unnecessary valuables, perishable items, or burdensome packaging. Include only the information required by the fulfillment provider.
5. **Coordinate the next step.** Explain whether an optional workshop, resource, or conversation is available. Delivery confirmation is not agreement to a meeting and should not trigger a presumptive sales message.
6. **Reconcile cost and outcome.** Include production, postage, address validation, returns, and staff time. Track receipt separately from useful response and subsequent commercial progression.

## Worked example

A hypothetical software team offers participants in an agreed planning workshop a printed process-mapping workbook. Recipients choose a business delivery address or the digital version. The package contains the workbook and session preparation notes, with no expensive gift.

Two packages are returned. The operator corrects the delivery process instead of counting all shipments as engagement. Workshop usefulness is assessed through completed maps and participant feedback, not the number of tracking numbers created.

## Stop conditions

Pause if addresses are unreliable, recipients question the relevance, or the item creates policy friction. A smaller welcomed send is more defensible than a large shipment whose primary outcome is waste. Retain fulfillment data only under the approved retention policy.

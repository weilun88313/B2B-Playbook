You have signed a reseller, but their team is not yet comfortable selling or supporting the product. Channel marketing helps a partner learn the customer problem, explain the offer, and deliver what was promised. Start with the full customer experience rather than another general pitch deck.

## Choose the channel role

Clarify whether the partner refers, resells, implements, supports, or combines those roles. Each arrangement changes pricing, customer ownership, qualification, and escalation. Do not assume that a referral agreement prepares a partner to deliver the product.

## Activate a small partner cohort

1. **Define the end-customer use case.** Specify fit, exclusions, required skills, and the outcome the partner can credibly promise.
2. **Agree the commercial model.** Document margin or fees, deal registration, discount authority, renewal ownership, and conflict resolution. Ensure the partner can make money after delivery effort.
3. **Build role-specific enablement.** Sellers need qualification and objection handling; implementers need setup and troubleshooting; support teams need escalation paths. A general pitch deck cannot replace all three.
4. **Practice a complete customer path.** Rehearse discovery, demonstration, quote, order, onboarding, and support with test data. Check where ownership or system access is unclear.
5. **Support the first opportunities.** Provide office hours or joint calls within a bounded support plan. Record repeated questions and turn them into reusable material.
6. **Review activation and customer outcomes.** Count partners that can execute the agreed motion, not just signed contracts. Inspect customer adoption, retention, support issues, and commercial contribution.

## Worked example

A fictional analytics vendor recruits regional consultancies as implementation resellers. The first cohort can demonstrate the product but struggles to scope data cleanup. The vendor adds a scoping worksheet and a technical review before quotes are finalized.

The intervention reduces the risk of underpriced delivery. Recruiting more partners before fixing the scoping gap would have multiplied the same customer problem. Partner count remains a secondary metric until the motion works.

## Review the relationship

Separate a partner's lack of activity from lack of capability or poor market fit. Ask what blocked the last real opportunity. Change training when knowledge is missing, economics when effort is unrewarded, and selection when the audience is wrong. Funding another campaign cannot solve every channel problem.

## Try it with your own work

Ask one partner to walk through their last opportunity. Find the step where they needed help—qualification, demonstration, scope, or support—and make that step easier.

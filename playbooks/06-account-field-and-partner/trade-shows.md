# Trade shows

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-30

A trade show is a time-bounded account-access program. Booth traffic and badge scans are **receipts**. Qualified next actions are the output. The scan proves they walked past. It does not prove a job, a seat, a trigger, or a week.

## Use this when

- Relevant accounts and buying seats concentrate at a known event.
- The team can research and pre-book before travel.
- The event creates access or evidence that cheaper channels cannot.

## Do not use this when

- “Everyone in the industry goes” is the main reason.
- The budget is committed before account access is tested.
- Badge volume is the success metric.

Use the [event-marketing playbook](event-marketing.md) first to decide attend, sponsor, exhibit, or skip.

## 40 / 20 / 40 operating heuristic

- **40% before:** account mapping, meeting booking, briefs, partner coordination, and logistics.
- **20% on site:** protected meetings, relevant conversations, capture, and daily review.
- **40% after:** conversation-specific follow-up, next-action execution, opportunity validation, and program review.

This is an effort-allocation heuristic, not an ROI formula.

## Before the show

1. Review exhibitor, attendee, speaker, sponsor, and partner data where permitted.
2. Classify accounts by ICP tier and required buying seats.
3. Assign one-to-one research to T1 accounts and a shared hypothesis to T2 accounts.
4. Pre-book qualified meetings before increasing booth or sponsorship spend.
5. Prepare a conversation brief for every scheduled meeting.
6. Define capture fields, owners, follow-up timing, and system-of-record rules.
7. Train booth and field staff on qualification, routing, and stop conditions.

## On site

- Protect scheduled meetings from general booth traffic.
- **CRM context before they walk up:** tier, last touch, open opportunity, who owns the account. A cold scan with no context is a new research problem, not a lead.
- Confirm context before explaining the product.
- Ask a short **qualification set you wrote**—not the scanner vendor’s defaults. Minimum: job they came to finish, current alternative, timing, buying seat, missing seat.
- Record **interaction signals** that are not the badge: dwell, second pass, asked for a walk, named a next step, brought a second person. A two-second scan and a twenty-minute working conversation are not the same row.
- Assign an owner and date while the buyer is present.
- Route partners, customers, job seekers, vendors, and unqualified scans appropriately.
- Run a short daily review while evidence is fresh.

Capture tools still have to pass [MarTech governance](../09-operations-pipeline-and-measurement/martech-governance.md): offline, custom fields, CRM write-back, and whether booth staff will actually use the app. Names start in [TOOLS.md](../../TOOLS.md) (booth capture). This page is the test.

## After the show — rank, do not spray

- Send follow-up from the actual conversation, not a generic event template.
- **Priority is evidence, not scan time.** Rank: pre-booked meeting with a next step → qualified conversation with a dated action → interaction signal plus ICP → receipt-only scan (nurture or suppress).
- Complete agreed actions before adding another sales touch.
- Add only qualified evidence to pipeline reporting. Receipts do not become opportunities.
- Review decision quality, meeting quality, committee coverage, next-action completion, and cost.
- Record which account or operational assumption changed.

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] The event decision is evidence-backed.
- [ ] Target accounts are tiered.
- [ ] Required buying seats are mapped.
- [ ] Qualified meetings are pre-booked.
- [ ] Every meeting has a brief and owner.
- [ ] Staff understand qualification and routing.
- [ ] Qualification questions are ours, not the scanner defaults.
- [ ] CRM context will be visible on the floor.
- [ ] Capture fields, interaction signals, and follow-up rules are ready.
- [ ] Post-show rank (not scan order) is written.
- [ ] Badge-data use and consent follow applicable requirements.
- [ ] The team can downgrade or cancel the format when evidence weakens.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Pre-booked qualified meetings | Tests access before the event |
| Qualified conversations | Counts buyer, problem, timing, or next-action evidence |
| Next-action completion | Agreed next actions completed / qualified conversations |
| Committee coverage | Required seats reached across target accounts |
| Event-sourced progression | Accounts reaching the next qualified decision |
| Cost per qualified next action | Total event cost / completed qualified next actions |
| Receipt-only share | Scans with no job or seat—must not enter pipeline |

## Common mistakes

- Buying a booth before testing account access.
- Treating every scan as a lead.
- Following up in scan order instead of evidence rank.
- Allowing unscheduled traffic to displace high-value meetings.
- Sending one generic follow-up to everyone.
- Claiming pipeline from meetings without qualified next actions.

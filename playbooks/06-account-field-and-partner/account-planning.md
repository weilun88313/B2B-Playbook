**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-29

An [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md) says who deserves attention. A [buying committee](../01-strategy-and-buyers/buying-committee.md) map says who must say yes. An **account plan** is the living file that answers: **what is going on in this company, what we will do in the next 30/60/90 days, and who owns each move.** Without it, “strategic account” is a label on a CRM record.

This is not a 40-page agency binder and not a close process. Papering and procurement stay outside this taxonomy. The plan exists so marketing, sales, and success share one story before they spend coordinated time on a named account.

Sales is a thinking process. The worksheet is a prompt. If the page is full and nobody can say *why they should solve with you, what they are trying to solve, how they solve it today, who the characters are, and when they must solve*, you have decorated a file. You have not planned.

## Use this when

- A named account is large enough that a single email sequence is not the system.
- An opportunity is stuck and the team cannot point to the next stage-based action.
- Marketing and sales disagree about “what is going on” in an account they both claim.
- You are about to spend coordinated time on a named account and the file is a logo list.

## Do not use this when

- You cannot name the ICP. Complete [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md) first.
- You still need the [first ten](../01-strategy-and-buyers/first-ten-customers.md) by hand. A plan for accounts you have never spoken to is fiction.
- You want a SWOT/PEST workshop to postpone asking for a meeting.
- Legal or a customer contract forbids the research you would put on the page. Stop; this is not a license to scrape.

## One rule

**The attack plan must come from the sales stages you actually run.** “Build a relationship” is not an action. “Get the economic buyer on a mutual close plan by Friday” is. If an action cannot be mapped to a stage, a date, and an owner, it does not belong on the plan.

## Operating method

### Step 1: one-page overview before the novel

Write the header so a stranger on the account could brief in two minutes:

- Account name, site, owner (rep), specialist if any
- Age of the opportunity (or of the relationship, if this is expansion)
- Stage, expected close date, expected amount—or “no live opp; land-and-expand”
- Last update date. A plan without a date is a grave.

Then five blocks. Empty is allowed. Invented color is not.

1. **Account.** Where they operate, what they sell, current customer-of-ours or not, competitive threats, partners already in the building.
2. **Opportunity (or expansion thesis).** The business outcome they named, the strategy they are using, the personal drivers of the people who care. Not your product pitch.
3. **Attack plan.** The specific moves that close the *largest honest* next contract. All of them from the stage the deal is in.
4. **Timed work.** Objectives, desired outcomes, activities, dates, names. This is the 30/60/90 (and 180 if the cycle is that long). Include a **last-90 lookback** so you do not relaunch the same failed move.
5. **Key contacts.** Programme owner, economic buyer, champion, operators. Business agenda *and* personal agenda. Existing relationship yes/no. Next action per person.

If you cannot fill (1) and (5) from [account research](../05-outbound-and-prospecting/account-research.md) and the committee map, you are not planning. You are guessing.

### Step 2: force the five thinking questions

Before you add frameworks, answer these in sentences the customer would recognize:

| Question | You are done when |
|---|---|
| **What** are they trying to solve? | A problem in their words, not a feature |
| **Why** solve with you? | Alternative named; [positioning](../02-product-marketing/positioning.md) holds |
| **How** do they solve it today? | Incumbent, spreadsheet, or “we don’t” |
| **Who** are the characters? | Seats, not a pile of titles |
| **When** must they solve? | A date with a reason (budget cycle, launch, risk event) |

Impact questions belong here: what happens if they do nothing; what happens if they pick the wrong path. If you cannot ask them live, write the ones you still owe.

### Step 3: map the landscape, then stop researching

A deeper strategic file is useful for a *few* accounts. It is malpractice as a default.

Useful, in order:

- **Landscape.** Company, product/service, size, properties, stack—enough to not sound lost.
- **History of the account.** What you already sold or failed to sell; services you could sell next; a vision in three SMART goals, not twelve.
- **Intake on a new engagement.** Overlaps landscape; designed for day one of a paid relationship, not a hunting fantasy.
- **Relationship map.** Name, position, influence, public profile if they have one, comments, next action. Six named humans beat a pretty org-chart graphic with nobody on it.
- **Resources that build the relationship.** Which paper, exec, or proof this *person* would forward—not a content dump.

Optional assessments (SWOT, PEST, social listening, brand, web analytics) are **diagnostics**. Run one if a decision is blocked. Do not run all of them because a template had a table of contents.

Agency-shaped extras (jobs-to-be-done brainstorms, “20 ideas in 20 minutes”) are idea generators. They do not replace the attack plan.

### Step 4: time-box the work

A 30-day plan that lists “nurture” is a slogan. Write:

- **30 days:** conversations and proofs you can actually book.
- **60 days:** committee seats you still lack; a mutual next step.
- **90 days:** a commercial event (pilot, paper, no-decision) you are willing to call.
- **180 days:** only if the cycle is honestly that long.
- **Last 90:** what you said you would do, and whether you did.

Each line: owner, date, why. Accountabilities without names are decorations.

### Step 5: keep a health check, not a shrine

On a living account, revisit:

- Who is the strongest ally, and can you speak candidly?
- Would they be a reference? Have you *asked*?
- Tone of the last month of conversations.
- How you might be more worth their time.
- How often you actually check in versus how often you think you do.

Risks and barriers go in a table: level, idea to overcome, owner, resource. An open risk with no owner is how accounts go quiet.

Opportunities (upsell, new product, advocacy) need an objective, a product, a timeline, and a status—same discipline as a new logo.

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] The header has a date and an owner.
- [ ] Attack-plan lines map to a real stage, a date, and a name.
- [ ] The five questions are answered in customer language.
- [ ] Committee seats are on the contact block, including unknowns.
- [ ] 30/60/90 is work, not nurture slogans; last-90 is honest.
- [ ] Optional assessments were run only to unblock a decision.
- [ ] Health and risks have owners.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Plan last-updated age | Dead files masquerading as strategy |
| Stage-mapped actions vs slogans | Whether the attack plan is real |
| Unknown required seats | Committee work still open |
| Last-90 completion | Whether the team does what it writes |
| Reference/advocacy asks on healthy accounts | Health check is not theater |

Do not count pages in a template, SWOT workshops completed, or “strategic” tags in the CRM as success.

## Common mistakes

- A beautiful landscape with no next meeting.
- Actions that cannot sit on a stage.
- Titles without personal agendas.
- Rerunning last quarter’s 30-day plan with new dates.
- PEST/SWOT as delay.
- Treating the plan as a sales-only secret so marketing runs a parallel story.
- Opportunity amount copied from a wish, not from what the champion can defend.

## Sources and evidence boundary

This is an owner-maintained operating synthesis. It is not a licensed account-planning product and not a substitute for customer permission or employment of a sales methodology.

The one-page header (account, opportunity, stage-based attack, timed work, key contacts), the five thinking questions plus a timed 30/60/90/180 and last-90 lookback, and the deeper landscape / relationship-map / health-check sequence are distilled from operator templates: a Pavilion summary account-planning sheet; a 2019 Catalyst Sale account-planning worksheet (their “sales is a thinking process” frame); and a strategic account-planning template circulated from Unstrategic / GAL-style agency practice (landscape, intake, optional SWOT/PEST, relationship map, risks, health questions). Those files are **method prompts**, not sources to copy, and not verified use cases here. Agency examples (CMS jokes, sample spend tables) are not imported. Named books and sites pointed from those materials are not abridged in this repository.

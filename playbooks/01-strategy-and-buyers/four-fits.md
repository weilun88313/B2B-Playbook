# Four Fits

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-30

[Product-market fit](product-market-fit.md) asks whether one segment loves it, pays, and starts to pull. This page asks the harder question: **are market, product, channel, and model the same company**—or four slides that refuse each other.

A team can have a product buyers want and still feel like they are pushing a boulder uphill. The usual diagnosis is “we lack growth tactics.” The more useful diagnosis is a missing fit: the product was not built for the channel that can actually reach the buyer, the channel cannot pay for itself at this price, or the market cannot support the model you are selling to the board.

This is not a $100M manifesto, not a Reforge course, and not permission to skip the [ICP](icp.md).

## Use this when

- Marketing is adding channels because “PMF is done.”
- Sales wants enterprise motion on a self-serve price, or PLG on a six-figure close.
- A new AI feature expanded the problem space and nobody revisited how you charge or how you get found.
- Growth feels heroic every week despite a team that “executes well.”

## Do not use this when

- There is no buyer and no first company. Stay in [idea validation](idea-validation.md) and [first ten customers](first-ten-customers.md).
- You are still arguing the PMF ladder for one segment. Write that evidence first.
- You want a channel mix menu. That is [channel strategy](../04-channels-and-distribution/channel-strategy.md)—after these four sentences agree.
- You want a price book. That is [pricing](../02-product-marketing/pricing-and-packaging.md). This page only asks whether that price can live with the channel.

## Words you will use

| Fit | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **Market–product** | A named segment desperately wants this job done. Start with the market, not the feature list. |
| **Product–channel** | The product is *built for* the way buyers will find it. Channels do not mold to a product you already shipped. |
| **Channel–model** | The economics of the channel fit the economics of the offer (ARPU can pay CAC, or the loop can). |
| **Model–market** | How this market already buys, times how many of them exist, can support the company you claim to be. |

Smooth sailing is all four clicking at once. Tugboat is executing “best practices” on a square peg.

## One rule

**Do not add a channel until you can say which of the other three fits it preserves—and which it would break.** “We’ll find distribution after PMF” is how a sales-assist product ends up on a viral bet, or a $8 seat ends up with a field team.

## Operating method

### Step 1: write four sentences, not a strategy deck

Fill the [four-fits card](../../templates/four-fits.md). Each sentence must name a fact you would be embarrassed to be wrong about:

1. *Which buyers, doing which job, already pay or would pay for this?*
2. *Which discovery path is this product actually shaped for—search, sales conversation, product-led invite, partner, event?*
3. *What does one successful customer pay, and what does it cost to get them through that path?*
4. *How does this market already buy, and does the math still work if we only capture a believable slice?*

If sentence 3 requires a miracle conversion rate, you do not have channel–model fit. You have a spreadsheet.

### Step 2: refuse the silo

Product and channel are designed together. A comparison-heavy evaluation product wants pages a champion can forward ([content strategy](../03-brand-story-and-content/content-strategy.md)) and a [pitch](../02-product-marketing/sales-enablement.md). A product that only works after a two-hour implementation will not be found by a self-serve loop. A $50k license will not be found by a meme.

If you change the product, revisit the channel. If you change the channel, revisit the product. Same for price.

### Step 3: plot the danger zone

Write last quarter’s **fully loaded cost to get one qualified conversation or one closed logo** next to **what they pay in year one**. If the channel only works at a price the market will not pay, pick a different channel—or a different market. Do not “optimize creative.”

Defaults you will override with your own log:

- High-touch outbound and field need ACV that can pay humans.
- Cheap or free distribution can carry a low ARPU—until the platform closes the gate.
- A mid-price that is too small for a hunter and too large for a card is how teams live in the $10–20k swamp.

### Step 4: treat AI as a simultaneous shock

AI can expand the problem, shrink the buyer (fewer seats), break free/freemium cost-to-serve, and move discovery from blue links into answers. That is not “add an AEO workstream.” It is a reason to **re-run all four sentences in the same week**. [SEO and AEO](../04-channels-and-distribution/seo-and-aeo.md) is the capture layer after this page still agrees.

Product-market fit was always a moment. The interval between “have it” and “lost it” is shorter when the buyer’s default tool is a model.

### Step 5: one change, then re-check the other three

Moving up-market, adding a PLG SKU, or standing up a partner motion is a **system** change. Most failures here are one-fit edits: a new channel on the old product, a new price on the old motion.

## Teaching fill (invented—not a customer)

A workflow tool sold by two founders. ACV ~$12k. Buyers find them on comparison pages. The board wants “TikTok plus an enterprise AE.”

| Fit | Fill |
|---|---|
| Market–product | Ops leads at 50–200 person B2B firms who are drowning in spreadsheet handoffs. Dated: 11 paid logos, 3 would be angry if we vanished. |
| Product–channel | Evaluation is a forwarded comparison URL + a 30-minute walk. Product has no invite loop. |
| Channel–model | One AE-shaped hire at this ACV is costume. Outbound can work if the founder still sells. TikTok cannot pay for this close. |
| Model–market | This slice can support a real company only if we stay in the comparison/outbound path—or we raise ACV and rebuild the product for a committee. |
| Break if we… | Hire a field team at $12k, or ship a free viral SKU that cannot become the paid seat. |

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] Four sentences are written on one card, not four decks.
- [ ] The product’s actual discovery path is named (not the path we wish we had).
- [ ] Year-one pay vs fully loaded path cost is a number, not a vibe.
- [ ] The market’s existing buying habit is named (card, inbound eval, outbound, procurement).
- [ ] Adding a channel names which other fit it would break.
- [ ] An AI or packaging change triggered a re-run of all four, or we wrote why it did not.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Path cost vs year-one ARPU | Channel–model, not a CAC slogan |
| Share of new logos from the path the product was built for | Whether we are living in a side channel |
| Time since the four sentences were re-dated | Staleness—especially after a model or packaging change |

Do not count “we have PMF” as a fit. Do not import a $100M threshold as your OKR unless that is actually the company you are building.

## Common mistakes

- Treating product-market fit as the only fit.
- Building the product, then shopping for a channel.
- Running a hunter motion on a price that cannot pay the hunter.
- Adding AI features without revisiting cost-to-serve and how buyers now search.
- Copying another company’s four-fit case (including a famous sales-product story) as your card.

## Sources and evidence boundary

This is an owner-maintained operating synthesis. It is not a venture-scale mandate and not a Reforge enrollment.

The four interlocking fits (market–product, product–channel, channel–model, model–market), the claim that products are built for channels rather than the reverse, the ARPU–CAC danger zone, and the warning that the fits must be revisited together draw on Brian Balfour’s Four Fits series ([original, 2017](https://brianbalfour.com/four-fits-growth-framework?ref=b2b-playbook); [AI-era update, 2025-09-25](https://blog.brianbalfour.com/p/the-four-fits-a-growth-framework?ref=b2b-playbook)). Those essays are **method prompts**, not a source to copy. $100M thresholds, Smooth Sailer / Tugboat labels, HubSpot Sales as a case, named AI companies, and platform-cycle stories stay with that author. They are not this library’s forecast or a command to pursue venture scale.

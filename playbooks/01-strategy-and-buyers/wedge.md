**Last reviewed:** 2026-09-03

[ICP](icp.md) says who may buy. A wedge is the **first niche you will actually win**: one audience plus one use case where the product is 10× better than the current way, then a planned expansion that uses that foothold instead of starting from scratch.

A homepage that markets the fundraise TAM is a pitch deck with CSS. Horizontal products still need a wedge. Vertical products still get too broad. Expansion that cannot name how the first niche helps the second is a second startup.

This is not a catalog of every segment you might serve later, not the [first ten](first-ten-customers.md) handmade ring, and not [positioning](../02-product-marketing/positioning.md)—positioning frames the wedge; it does not pick it.

## Use this when

- The product *could* serve many seats or industries, and the site already tries.
- Content and outbound feel generic because the audience is “GTM teams” or “mid-market SaaS.”
- Leadership wants the vision market on the homepage while sales still cannot name a 10× job.
- You are about to add a second ICP with no proof the first one is converting off the alternative.

## Do not use this when

- ICP is empty. Finish [ICP](icp.md) first.
- You still need the first ~10 logos. Stay in [first ten customers](first-ten-customers.md). Handmade rings are how you *find* a wedge, not a reason to skip one.
- Product-market fit for this niche is unproven. Climb [product-market fit](product-market-fit.md) before you scale the wedge’s engine.
- The request is a TAM slide for investors. GTM TAM is narrower than the pitch TAM on purpose.

## Words you will use

| Word | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **Wedge** | Audience segment **plus** use case where you are 10× better than the named alternative |
| **Pitch TAM** | The market you tell investors. Not the homepage, not Q1 spend |
| **GTM TAM** | Accounts you will put in the CRM and try to reach this year |
| **Expand** | Same audience, new use case—or same use case, new audience. The first niche must help |
| **Stuck** | Still treating the first niche as the whole company after it is converting |

## One rule

**Win a niche, then use it to get the next one.** If the second segment needs a new story, a new engine, and a new proof set, you did not have a wedge. You had two bets.

## Operating method

### Step 1: name the 10× pair, not the vision

Write one sentence: *we win ___ (seat / company type) when they are trying to ___ (use case), because we are 10× better than ___ (named alternative).*

The alternative is a tool, a vendor, or a process they already run—not “doing nothing” unless that is truly the comparator. If you cannot name a 10×, you do not have a wedge. You have a feature list. [Positioning](../02-product-marketing/positioning.md) then frames that pair; it does not invent a wider market to make the sentence nicer.

Horizontal product: still pick one pair. Vertical product: still pick one use case inside the vertical. Do not market every workflow the roadmap might touch.

### Step 2: put the wedge’s GTM TAM in the CRM

Narrow the list until you can actually work it. Get those accounts (and later, contacts) into the CRM. [ABM strategy](../06-account-field-and-partner/abm-strategy.md) is how you **tier** that universe; this step only says the universe is the wedge, not the pitch deck.

If you cannot list the accounts, the wedge is still a slogan.

### Step 3: make fuel and engine for that pair only

[Content strategy](../03-brand-story-and-content/content-strategy.md) writes pages this niche would forward. [Channel strategy](../04-channels-and-distribution/channel-strategy.md) picks the engine that actually reaches them:

| Motion | Typical fit with a tight wedge |
|---|---|
| Outbound | Sales-assist; value-add to a named pain |
| Inbound | Self-serve / PLG; specific questions, not “what is X” |
| Ecosystem | Partners and rooms that already have this seat |

The [homepage](../07-website-and-conversion/homepage.md) speaks to the **primary** wedge. Other audiences get their own URL later—not a second headline in the hero. Onboarding and enablement should match the same pair.

Inbound that shows up from a *different* seat is a clue for the next wedge. It is not a reason to mash the hero this quarter.

### Step 4: plan the expansion before you are stuck—and do not expand on a wish

While the first pair is still the spend priority, write the next move:

- **Same audience, new use case** (land and expand inside the logo).
- **Same use case, new audience** (the first niche’s proof, partners, or content must transfer).

Vertical products usually expand use cases inside the industry. Horizontal products usually add audiences once one use case is believed. Either way: the first niche has to **accelerate** the second. If you would be starting from zero—new proof, new rooms, new alternative—the second bet is testing, not expansion. Rank it in [ICP](icp.md) maturity; do not fund it like core.

Do not wait until the first niche is “done.” Do not abandon it the week a board member wants a bigger TAM.

## Teaching fill (invented—not a customer)

Queue software. Horizontal product. Pitch story is “every ops team.”

| Field | Fill |
|---|---|
| Wedge | Customer-ops leads at B2B SaaS (~200–1,000) running security questionnaires from a shared inbox |
| 10× vs | Spreadsheet + drive + hero who remembers the SLA |
| GTM TAM | Named accounts that post security/ops jobs or lost a deal on questionnaire SLA |
| Will not market this quarter | “GTM platform for every revenue team.” Agency owners. HR onboarding |
| Next move (planned) | Same seat, vendor-review use case—only after questionnaire win rate is dated |
| Stuck check | Homepage still says questionnaires; paid still points at that URL |

Public illustrations Kramer used for *expansion that used the first niche* (cap table → equity platform; restaurant booking → POS; payments API → infrastructure) stay **her** examples on that date—not templates to copy, and not this library’s customers.

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] [ICP](icp.md) canvas exists; this page does not invent a second profile.
- [ ] The pair is audience **plus** use case, not a category name.
- [ ] The alternative is named; “10×” would fail if we swapped our logo for theirs.
- [ ] Homepage and primary campaign speak to this pair, not the pitch TAM.
- [ ] Accounts in the wedge can be listed or loaded.
- [ ] Next expansion is written, and the first niche is a real input to it.
- [ ] A second ICP is ranked testing until this one converts off the alternative.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Conversion vs alternative | Wins that displaced the named workaround, not “new logo” |
| Hero match | Homepage conversations from the wedge seat vs everyone else |
| Transfer | Second niche that reused proof, partners, or content from the first |
| Spread | Campaigns this quarter that cannot name the pair |

Do not count TAM slides, category announcements, or “we also serve” bullets as a wedge.

## Common mistakes

- Marketing the investor deck: every future audience, vision language, features you cannot win on yet.
- Treating wedge as “we are vertical SaaS” (horizontal products still need one).
- Treating wedge as “we picked an industry” with no use case.
- Expanding by starting a second brand story.
- Getting stuck in the first niche after it is converting, with no next move.
- Loading the whole pitch TAM into ABM and calling it focus.

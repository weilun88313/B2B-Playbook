# Buying committee

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-24

A B2B purchase is made by a system of people with different incentives, authority, and risk—not by a single persona.

## Use this when

- An account has an interested contact but no visible approval path.
- Marketing and sales use “decision-maker” as a catch-all role.
- Opportunities stall in security, finance, legal, procurement, or implementation.

## Do not use this when

- The account fails the [ICP](icp.md) or has no meaningful trigger.
- The team is inventing names or political relationships without evidence.
- The map is being treated as a static org chart rather than a decision hypothesis.

## Core seats

| Seat | Role in the decision | Typical question |
|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer | Owns the business result or budget authority | Is this worth the cost and organizational change? |
| Champion | Builds internal momentum and explains the decision | Can I carry this process when the seller is absent? |
| Technical buyer | Evaluates security, data, architecture, or implementation | Can this work safely inside our environment? |
| Blocker | Can delay or stop the change | What risk, loss of control, or competing priority does this create? |
| Operator | Performs the work and experiences the workflow | Does this improve the real task? |
| Procurement / legal | Controls commercial and contractual process | Can the organization buy this under acceptable terms? |

One person may hold several seats. A seat may also be shared across a committee.

## Operating method

### Step 1: begin with the decision

Write the business decision, the current alternative, the trigger, and the expected decision date. A committee cannot be mapped without knowing what it is deciding.

### Step 2: map seats, not only titles

A CFO is not always the economic buyer, and a user is not automatically the champion. Assign a seat only when behavior or evidence supports it.

### Step 3: separate fact from hypothesis

| Evidence state | Example |
|---|---|
| Fact | The security lead joined the technical review |
| Observation | Procurement owns vendor onboarding in similar accounts |
| Assumption | The CPO may control budget |
| To validate | Ask who signs and who can stop implementation |

### Step 4: identify the missing path

For every unknown seat, write who might know and what action will resolve the gap. Do not ask the champion to introduce every person at once; connect the request to a real decision or risk.

### Step 5: update after every material interaction

Record changes in influence, objections, timing, approval, and implementation ownership. A committee map that does not change after discovery is probably decorative.

### Relationship questions

- Who owns the result?
- Who carries the daily work?
- Who owns the current alternative?
- Who can approve budget?
- Who can stop security, legal, procurement, or implementation?
- Who must explain the change when the seller is absent?

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] The business decision and trigger are explicit.
- [ ] Seat assignments have evidence.
- [ ] Unknown seats have discovery actions.
- [ ] The champion has access, credibility, and motivation.
- [ ] The economic buyer is not assumed from title alone.
- [ ] Technical, procurement, legal, and implementation paths are visible when relevant.
- [ ] The map was updated after the latest material interaction.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Committee completeness | Qualified accounts with required seats named or paired with a discovery action |
| Champion validation | Opportunities where the champion has taken a verifiable internal action |
| Single-thread rate | Opportunities dependent on one active contact |
| Seat progression | New required seats reached after each decision milestone |
| Late-blocker rate | Opportunities first discovering a required blocker after commercial intent |

## Common mistakes

- Calling any friendly contact a champion.
- Mapping titles without mapping the decision.
- Ignoring operators because they lack budget authority.
- Treating procurement or security as paperwork instead of decision participants.
- Hiding unknowns to make the opportunity appear complete.

## Sources and evidence boundary

This is an owner-maintained operating synthesis. Role names are working categories, not universal organizational truth. Validate them against the account's actual process, policies, and participants.

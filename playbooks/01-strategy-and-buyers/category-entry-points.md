**Published:** 2026-09-12 · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

Category entry points are buying situations that can prompt someone to think of a category and recall possible providers. “We have grown into three offices and access permissions are getting messy” is a situation. “Innovative identity platform” is a vendor description.

## Use situations to guide memory-building

Use this guide when brand activity has a consistent visual identity but no clear connection to when buyers might need the company. It also helps decide which use cases deserve repeated communication. A category entry point is not a verified buying signal for a particular person, and it does not authorize outreach.

## Find and choose the situations

1. Ask category buyers about recent moments when they considered a solution. Include people who bought competitors or decided to wait. Capture what happened, where, with whom, under what time pressure and for what purpose.
2. Group equivalent situations without erasing important differences. Separate a trigger such as an audit from a requirement such as data residency. Both matter, but they play different roles in a decision.
3. Evaluate relevance and deliverability. Can your offer help in this situation today? What proof would establish that? Reject situations that need a product capability you do not have.
4. Estimate how broadly the situation occurs using appropriate research. Do not turn the frequency of mentions in a handful of sales calls into a population estimate. Record how the sample was recruited.
5. Select a manageable set and connect each to consistent brand cues. Vary the story and format while repeating the association. A buyer should recognize the company without every piece having identical copy.
6. Measure the association over time. Use stable prompts, sampling and category definitions when asking which brands come to mind. Keep awareness, relevant recall and immediate sales response separate.

## Worked example

A fictional access-management company chooses “opening another office” and “preparing for a customer security review.” For each it creates one useful explanation, a credible implementation example and a route to explore the product. Both use the same recognizable visual and verbal identity.

The company does not call every office announcement proof of an active purchase. It uses the situation to make future communication more relevant, while account-level timing remains a separate research question. If buyers remember the advice but cannot identify the company, distribution alone has not built the intended association.

## Situation brief

| Question | Record |
|---|---|
| What happens? | A specific circumstance in buyer language |
| Who experiences it? | Roles and operating context |
| Why our company? | Current capability and supporting evidence |
| What repeats? | Recognizable cues and core association |
| Where will buyers encounter it? | Reach plan and useful formats |
| How will we learn? | Research question, sample and review period |

## Review before expanding

Check whether the selected situations are too narrow to matter or too broad to distinguish a real need. Do not abandon consistent cues because an internal team is bored. Change the situation strategy when evidence changes, and use a separate short-term scoreboard for active demand.

Choose one task and finish its working file before opening another chapter. Each path below combines a guide, a private copy of a template, and a concrete review point.

**Reading edited:** 2026-09-11

The examples are fictional teaching examples, not customer results or recommended performance targets. Replace the sample assumptions with evidence from your own work.

## Find your first customers

Use this path when you have a product idea but need to decide which buyers to approach first.

1. Read the [ICP field test](01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md#10-minute-field-test). Write down the business problem, the person responsible, and what they do today.
2. Make a private copy of the [wedge card](../templates/wedge.md). Choose one audience and one use case; name the alternatives and the boundaries of the test.
3. Use [first ten customers](01-strategy-and-buyers/first-ten-customers.md) to plan conversations that can challenge those assumptions. Record what people actually say separately from your interpretation.

**Example wedge card:** A fictional software team explores appointment coordination for independent repair shops.

| Field | Illustrative entry |
|---|---|
| Audience | The owner or service manager who schedules repair appointments |
| Use case | Keep appointment changes visible to the staff handling calls |
| Named alternative | A shared calendar and handwritten notes; confirm this in interviews |
| Advantage to test | Staff can find the latest appointment status without asking the owner; unproven |
| Boundary | Explore appointment coordination first; exclude invoicing and inventory |
| Next action | Ask a relevant manager to walk through a recent schedule change and its consequences |

**Ready for review when:** the card names an audience, a job, an alternative, supporting evidence, unanswered questions, and the next conversation. Do not claim validation or a performance advantage until the evidence supports it.

## Prepare and review an outreach test

Use this path when you know the intended buyer and need a small, explainable outreach plan.

1. Read [account research](05-outbound-and-prospecting/account-research.md). Record why each account fits and which facts support the proposed message.
2. Fill a private copy of the [message-market-fit card](../templates/message-market-fit.md). Define the offer, the reason to respond, and what you need to learn.
3. Choose [cold email](05-outbound-and-prospecting/cold-email.md) or [LinkedIn outbound](05-outbound-and-prospecting/linkedin-outbound.md). Use one suitable route first; add a [multichannel sequence](05-outbound-and-prospecting/multichannel-sequence.md) only when the extra step has a purpose.

**Example outreach brief:** The same fictional repair-shop team prepares a message after a manager has described coordination problems in a discovery conversation.

| Field | Illustrative entry |
|---|---|
| Evidence | The manager described staff checking with them after appointment changes |
| Offer | Review one recent schedule change together and show a relevant workflow if useful |
| Draft | “You mentioned that staff check with you when appointments move. Would it be useful to walk through one recent change together and see whether a shared status view would help?” |
| Unknown | Whether the problem is frequent enough to justify a change |
| Review record | Contact basis, message, date, response, objection, and agreed next action |

**Ready for review when:** each message has a verifiable reason, an appropriate contact route, a clear ask, and an owner. After an authorized test, separate substantive replies, agreed next steps, declines, and delivery failures. Use the evidence to revise the offer; silence alone does not explain why it failed.

## Plan a trade-show follow-up workflow

Use this path when deciding how an event should contribute to specific account conversations.

1. Read [event marketing](06-account-field-and-partner/event-marketing.md). State the audience, account objective, effort, and decision that would justify attending.
2. Use [trade shows](06-account-field-and-partner/trade-shows.md) to plan qualification questions, roles, and follow-up before the event.
3. Make a private copy of the [show capture sheet](../templates/show-capture.md). Record the business context and agreed next action for each conversation.

**Example capture record:** A fictional team meets a service-operations manager at an industry event.

| Field | Illustrative entry |
|---|---|
| Business job | Coordinate appointment changes across staff |
| Evidence | The manager describes a recent handoff problem; the team has not verified its frequency |
| Missing information | Who else approves a workflow change and which system holds the schedule |
| Agreed action | Send the requested workflow example the next business day; sales owner follows up |
| Rank | Next-step, because an action and timing were agreed |
| Pipeline treatment | Record the conversation and action; do not count a badge scan as an opportunity |

**Ready for review when:** the event plan identifies target accounts, responsibilities, qualification questions, and a follow-up process. After the event, each prioritized record has evidence, an owner, and a dated next action. Review whether those actions happened before attributing commercial results to the event.

## Tell us what happened

If a template helped, describe the decision or output it supported. Remove customer names, contact details, and confidential working data before sharing.

# Pipeline

Define the market as accounts and people, not as a list of anonymous
traffic.

## ICP

Write four fields. If any is only “SMB / mid-market / enterprise,” it is not
done.

- Industry and use case
- Company size that can actually buy (budget owner, not headcount theater)
- Trigger that makes this quarter different from last quarter
- Disqualifiers (who we will not sell)

## Buying committee

Every live opportunity should name:

| Seat | Job in the deal |
|---|---|
| Economic buyer | Signs or blocks budget |
| Champion | Needs a win inside their team |
| Technical buyer | Can fail you on data, security, or integration |
| Blocker | Incumbent owner, legal, security, or operational resistance |

If you only have a champion, you have a conversation, not a deal.

## Opportunity definition

An opportunity is real when all of these are true:

- Named account
- Named next meeting or next event
- A problem stated in their words
- A close date tied to a business calendar (show, quarter, renewal)

Lead stages should describe observable buying progress. Do not promote a
contact to an opportunity merely because they opened an email, downloaded
a file, or attended an event.

## Account tiers

Use a small number of tiers with an explicit reason:

- **Tier 1:** high fit, clear trigger, research individually.
- **Tier 2:** good fit, weaker trigger, use a relevant segment hypothesis.
- **Tier 3:** possible fit, low evidence, keep out of active sequences.

The tier is a resource-allocation decision, not a judgment about the
company's importance.

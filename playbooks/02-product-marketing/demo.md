# Demo

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-29

A demo that “went well” is not evidence. Feelings do not close deals. **Execution does.** If you cannot say which seats were in the room, which discovery pain each click served, and what meeting you booked next, you ran a tour. You did not run a demo.

This page is the product walk **after** the buyer has agreed a [perfect world](sales-enablement.md). It is not the first meeting. If they have not agreed the insight and the approaches, do not open the product. You will spend the hour answering feature questions they did not earn.

## Use this when

- Recorded demos “sound fine” and still die as no-decision.
- Reps start with login and hope the buyer infers why it matters.
- Managers coach vibe (“more energy”) instead of a scored list of what happened.
- You are about to let an AE demo without a written discovery.

## Do not use this when

- There is no positioning and no pitch. Stay in [positioning](positioning.md) and [sales enablement](sales-enablement.md).
- The motion is truly self-serve and nobody should be walking the product live. That is [channel strategy](../04-channels-and-distribution/channel-strategy.md).
- You need a close process. Papering stays outside this taxonomy.

## One rule

**If you cannot connect a click to a pain they said, do not show it.** Most objections are born in features the prospect never asked for. Discovery is the gate. No disco, no demo.

## Operating method

### Step 1: decide who must be in the room

A demo for the champion alone is often a delayed no. Name the seats from the [buying committee](../01-strategy-and-buyers/buying-committee.md). Before the call, reach the people you have never met—not to pitch, to make the room less cold. During the call, use names. A silent economic buyer is not “aligned.” They are absent.

### Step 2: set the stage in their language

Before any click:

- Recap the goals they named.
- Recap what is in the way.
- Recap what you heard in discovery—out loud, so they can correct you.
- Paint a dated future (“imagine it is March and the team uses this daily—what is different?”).
- Stake the meeting: you want them fired enough to sponsor the next room, or honest enough to say you missed. Fair?
- One similar proof, then: here is how this helps **you**.

If this block is still in vendor language, you are not ready to share a screen.

### Step 3: show the strongest proof first

Start where they can say “that is the job.” Orient before every new surface: what we are looking at, whose pain it solves, why this click. Run a **so-what** stack until you reach a business consequence they own (time → capacity → the number they are paid on)—not “it automates reporting” as the last line.

After each key path: a short customer story they can remember, then a question. Not “any questions?” See the question list in the [working file](../../templates/demo-scorecard.xlsx).

### Step 4: end with a next meeting, not a vibe

Ask what excited them most—and **why**. If something fell flat, ask where you missed. Leave with a dated next step on the calendar. “I’ll send a recap” is not a next step.

### Step 5: score the recording, then coach one drill

Managers score every scored line, or the average is a lie. 1 = missed · 2 = grazed · 3 = weak attempt · 4 = solid · 5 = masterclass. An analyst stops at the number. A coach writes **keep / stop / one drill for the next demo**. Do not paste a generic AI prompt over this list and call it enablement. The list *is* the rubric.

## Teaching fill (invented—not a customer)

Mid-market analytics. Discovery said month-end close is a three-tool stitch. Champion + VP Finance on the call. AE had emailed the VP the day before.

| Field | Fill |
|---|---|
| People | Both seats present. VP greeted by name. Champion talking; VP asked to react twice. |
| Stage | Recap: “one number finance will trust.” Pain: spreadsheet heroics. Future: March close in one sitting. Stake: sponsor a CFO 30-min or say we missed. |
| During | Opened on the close pack, not admin settings. Each click tied to “you said Thursday is a war.” So-what to “CFO stops asking for a side file.” Story: unnamed peer, same close pain. Question after each path. |
| End | “What excited you?” → the audit trail. Next: 45 minutes with security on Thursday. |
| Score | Stage and during strong; people only 3 because a silent director joined late and was never brought in. Drill: 60-second name-and-role open. |

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] Discovery notes exist; the demo is not a fishing trip.
- [ ] Required seats were invited; missing seats are named, not hoped.
- [ ] Stage recap is in customer language.
- [ ] First click is the strongest relevant proof, not the product home.
- [ ] Every path has a so-what to a consequence they own.
- [ ] “Any questions?” is banned; real questions are written.
- [ ] A next meeting is the exit, or a written no.
- [ ] The recording will be scored against the same list.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Score by section (not overall vibe) | Where coaching should go |
| Next-meeting rate from demo | Whether the hour produced a step |
| No-decision after “great demo” | Tour vs choice |
| Features shown that were not in discovery | Self-inflicted objections |

Do not count demo length, decks shipped, or “they seemed excited” as enablement outcomes.

## Common mistakes

- Demo as the first meeting.
- Login → features until time dies.
- Showing the thing they did not mention.
- Stopping the so-what at “saves time.”
- “Any questions?” as the close.
- Coaching energy instead of the list.
- Copying a 21-line scorecard’s *sample phrases* as a script.

## Sources and evidence boundary

This is an owner-maintained operating synthesis. It is not a licensed demo methodology and not a guarantee of close.

The insistence on scoring what happened (not how it felt), discovery as a gate, people / stage / during / end as sections, so-what to a business consequence, story-plus-question after a path, banning “any questions?”, and coaching as keep / stop / one drill are distilled from an operator demo scorecard and question list (spreadsheet; includes an optional recording-coach prompt). That file is a **method prompt**, not a source to copy. Sample talk tracks and the recording-coach prompt are not imported as required scripts. “Book a meeting from a meeting” is ordinary pipeline hygiene here, not a branded close.

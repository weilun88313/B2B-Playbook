# Positioning

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-24

Positioning is the shortest credible explanation of why a specific buyer should change from the current alternative.

## Use this when

- The homepage cannot explain the buyer and outcome in ten seconds.
- Every salesperson tells a different story.
- Comparison pages list features but ignore the current alternative and switching cost.

## Do not use this when

- The ICP is empty. Complete [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md) first.
- The team wants a positioning document to substitute for product evidence.
- The current alternative is described only as “the traditional way.”

## Operating method

### Step 1: write the trigger, not the feature

A trigger is observable: a deadline, renewal, event, hiring pattern, regulation, incident, or failed audit. A feature is not a trigger.

### Step 2: complete one positioning sentence

> When [specific buyer] faces [trigger], [category] helps them [business result] without [critical cost or trade-off].

Then answer:

1. Why is the problem expensive, urgent, or strategically important?
2. Who owns the outcome and who carries the daily work?
3. What is the current alternative, including doing nothing?
4. What evidence makes a change credible?

For executive buyers, apply the board-level test: what one sentence would they use to explain the change to a CEO or COO? The answer should describe a strategic advantage, not an AI task list.

### Step 3: run the homepage 10-second test

A reader who never opens the blog should still identify:

- the product category;
- the buyer or operating context;
- the current alternative;
- the business result;
- one clear next step;
- one likely operational constraint, such as security, data, deployment, integration, or implementation effort.

### Step 4: build comparisons only for real decisions

Create a comparison page only when the alternative appears repeatedly in sales conversations. Include where the alternative is stronger. Refusing to name its strengths makes the page less credible and less useful to a champion.

### Step 5: synchronize the spoken version

Turn the same positioning into a 20-second verbal story:

> Trigger → crack in the alternative → result → one confirmation question.

The homepage, first outbound paragraph, and demo opening should share this structure.

### Comparison-page outline

1. **Decision:** “[Product] vs [alternative] for [specific job]”
2. **Short answer:** who should choose each option
3. **Decision context:** trigger, buyer, and current process
4. **Criteria:** outcome, implementation, workflow ownership, data/security, total operating cost
5. **Honest trade-offs:** where each option is stronger
6. **Switching path:** what changes and what remains
7. **Proof:** dated, verifiable evidence
8. **Next step:** one decision-specific action

## Homepage checklist

- [ ] A new visitor can identify the category.
- [ ] The buyer or operating context is explicit.
- [ ] The current alternative is recognizable.
- [ ] The business result is more prominent than the feature list.
- [ ] The page gives one clear next step.
- [ ] The hero avoids undefined superlatives.
- [ ] Proof is specific and verifiable.
- [ ] A likely blocker can find the relevant constraint.
- [ ] The same positioning can open an outbound conversation or demo.

## Metrics

| Metric | How to use it |
|---|---|
| Message recall | Ask a new teammate or prospect to repeat what the product is for after one exposure |
| Qualified CTA rate | Qualified visitors or target accounts taking the intended next step / qualified visitors exposed |
| Alternative specificity | Opportunities where the current tool or process is named / qualified opportunities |
| Sales-language consistency | Calls or emails using trigger → alternative → result / reviewed sample |

Do not count a completed messaging workshop, slogan volume, unqualified traffic, or curiosity clicks as positioning outcomes.

## Common mistakes

- Using a slogan as a substitute for a market decision.
- Naming the buyer but not the current alternative.
- Leading with features that do not explain why change matters now.
- Claiming differentiation without verifiable proof.
- Allowing the homepage, outbound message, and sales story to contradict one another.

## Sources and evidence boundary

This is an owner-maintained operating synthesis. Positioning claims must remain inside the available product, customer, security, and performance evidence.

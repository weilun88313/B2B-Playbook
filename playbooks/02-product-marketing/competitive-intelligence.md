**Last reviewed:** 2026-09-02

[Positioning](positioning.md) decides the frame. This page decides **what the company is allowed to know—and ship—when an alternative walks into the deal.**

A 40-page battlecard nobody opens is a performance. A scrape of every homepage in the category is a hobby. Intelligence is a **living card** sales will actually paste: where they win, where we win, what we will not say, and the URL a champion can forward.

This is not [account research](../05-outbound-and-prospecting/account-research.md) (one named account). It is not [ecosystem](../06-account-field-and-partner/ecosystem.md) (complements you might grow with). Complements still belong on the map so you do not treat a partner as an enemy.

## Use this when

- Sales keeps meeting the same alternative and invents a different story each time.
- The comparison page is a feature grid that always crowns us.
- Leadership wants “a competitive program” with no list from live deals.
- An AI brief is being treated as a fact because it filled every cell.

## Do not use this when

- Positioning is empty. Finish [positioning](positioning.md) first.
- You need one account’s trigger and seat. That is [account research](../05-outbound-and-prospecting/account-research.md).
- You need partner economics. That is [ecosystem](../06-account-field-and-partner/ecosystem.md).
- The request is a win/loss theater with no dated quotes.

## Words you will use

| Word | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **Alternative** | What they would keep using: incumbent, status quo, build, or a named vendor from live short lists |
| **Card** | One alternative, one page: where they win, where we win, what we will not claim, the forwardable URL |
| **Canonical** | The vendor’s current public URL, docs, and pricing page—not a blog recap or a model’s memory |
| **Blank** | Unknown. Better than a confident guess |
| **Complement** | A player who can help us grow. Not filed as a competitor by default |

## One rule

**Live short lists first. Canonical URLs second. Blank over guess.** If sales has not lost to it or put it on a short list this quarter, it does not get a card. If you cannot open the source, you do not write the cell.

## Operating method

### Step 1: steal the list from deals, not from a radar slide

Sit with the last ten serious conversations. Write only alternatives that appeared. Status quo counts. A famous logo the team never meets does not.

Cap the working set. Three to five cards beat a matrix of twenty. [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md) maturity still applies: core ICP alternatives get depth; testing ICP alternatives stay on a watch list.

### Step 2: separate research from the card

Product-marketing research (audience, market, product) is ongoing. The card is the synthesis sales can use. Do not paste the research into the pitch.

For each alternative, collect only what changes a conversation:

- How they describe who it is for, what it is, why it is better (their homepage four-liner).
- Price shape, if public—[pricing page](../07-website-and-conversion/pricing-page.md) rules still apply to *their* page as evidence, not as a command to copy.
- Where a champion already says they are stronger.
- Where we actually win today, with proof we can date.
- Complements in the same job (who we should not attack).

### Step 3: use sources that survive a spot-check

Order:

1. Canonical URLs (homepage, docs, pricing, changelog) with the date you opened them.
2. A customer or seller quote you can attribute.
3. A third-party page that already shows up in [SEO and AEO](../04-channels-and-distribution/seo-and-aeo.md) answers—if it is wrong, that is a fact about the market, not a fact about the product.

Rules:

- **Blank over guess.** An empty cell is honest. A model-filled cell is a claim.
- **Spot-check** anything an assistant drafts against the live URL before it reaches sales.
- Do not treat a scrape of 50 columns as a program. Columns you will not use are inventory, not intelligence.
- Test the method on **us** first: if our own public pages would fail the same pass, fix [messaging](messaging.md) and the site before you grade others.

### Step 4: write the card sales will paste

One card per alternative:

- When to use this card (the sentence a seller hears).
- Where they win—say it out loud. A card that cannot admit a loss is a brochure.
- Where we win, with proof.
- What we will not say (roadmap vapor, invented percentages, “they are dying”).
- The [comparison page](../07-website-and-conversion/comparison-page.md) URL, or “not this quarter.”
- Owner and review date (90 days or the next lost-deal cluster).

Ship the comparison URL only when the alternative is repeating. [Positioning](positioning.md#comparison-page-outline) still owns the outline.

### Step 5: refuse two superstitions

**Radar completeness:** a full category map is not a decision. **AI completeness:** a filled spreadsheet is not evidence. Intelligence that does not change a talk track, a page, or a disqualifier is a report.

## Teaching fill (invented—not a customer)

Sales-assist. Repeating alternative: shared inbox + spreadsheet. Named vendor only if it appeared.

| Field | Fill |
|---|---|
| Alternative | Shared inbox + SLA spreadsheet (status quo). Named help desk only if it showed up twice this quarter. |
| Canonical | Their help-center URL / dated. Status quo: our own call notes. |
| They win | Zero procurement; everyone already has email. |
| We win | Queue health is a named job; spreadsheet cannot show SLA without a war room. Dated: last three wins said so. |
| Will not say | “Email is dead.” Their uptime. A share we did not measure. |
| Forward | Comparison URL once the outline admits when status quo still wins. |

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] The list came from live short lists or losses, not a wish-radar.
- [ ] Each card has canonical URLs with dates, or an explicit blank.
- [ ] Where they win is written in language sales will say.
- [ ] Where we win has dated proof, or the line is cut.
- [ ] No invented share, roadmap, or “they are dying.”
- [ ] Complements are labeled, not filed as enemies by default.
- [ ] A model-drafted cell was spot-checked against the live page.
- [ ] Comparison pages exist only for repeating alternatives.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Card use | Times a seller pastes the card or comparison URL into an evaluation thread |
| List truth | Alternatives on cards / alternatives named in last ten serious deals |
| Stale cards | Cards past review date with no re-open of the canonical URL |
| Blank rate | Cells left unknown vs filled by guess |

Do not count battlecard page-count, scrape columns, or “we have a competitive Slack channel.”

## Common mistakes

- Building cards for vendors sales never meets.
- A comparison that cannot admit when the alternative wins.
- Treating complements as competitors.
- Pasting an AI brief into the deck without opening the source.
- Updating the radar weekly and the talk track never.
- Using win/loss adjectives (“they are expensive”) with no quote.
- Letting product-gap reviews replace “why we win today”—that stays on [positioning](positioning.md).

## Sources and evidence boundary

This is an owner-maintained operating synthesis. It is not a scraping product and not a command to copy a vendor’s page.

Tight lists from live short lists, “where they win,” and “position the way you sell” already live on [positioning](positioning.md), from April Dunford ([Lenny’s Newsletter, 2026-03-10](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/a-guide-to-advanced-b2b-positioning?ref=b2b-playbook)).

Product-marketing research as a separate job from the positioning statement, and the warning not to dump the research onto the homepage, draw on Emily Kramer ([MKT1, 2024-06-12](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/positioning-guide?ref=b2b-playbook)). Canonical URLs first, blank over guess, spot-check, and “test the method on yourself before you scale” draw on Kramer’s research playbook ([MKT1, 2026-05-06](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/state-of-marketing-report-part-3-how-to-research-in-claude-code?ref=b2b-playbook)). Complements vs competitors follow the ecosystem map already cited on [ecosystem](../06-account-field-and-partner/ecosystem.md). Paid battlecard generators, 50-column scrapes, and template packs are not reproduced here. Sample percentages in MKT1’s 100-company reports are **their** scrape, not this library’s baseline.

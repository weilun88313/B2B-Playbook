**Last reviewed:** 2026-09-02

[Positioning](positioning.md) is the market decision: who the product is for, against which alternative, why change now. Messaging is the **hierarchy that applies that decision** to a seat, a stage, and a surface without inventing a second story.

A positioning worksheet that never becomes a homepage line, a first-meeting opener, and an outbound paragraph is a workshop. Copy that forgets the alternative is a slogan. This page sits between them.

This is not brand strategy, not a tone-of-voice workshop, and not the [sales pitch](sales-enablement.md). Those inherit this hierarchy.

## Use this when

- Positioning exists, but the homepage, outbound, and deck each describe a different product.
- The hero tries to speak to every seat and every ICP at once.
- Campaigns cannot name which perception they move.
- Sales rewrites the story every call because the “messaging doc” is a slide of features.

## Do not use this when

- Positioning is empty. Finish [positioning](positioning.md) first.
- You need the first-meeting talk track. That is [sales enablement](sales-enablement.md).
- You need which URLs to write. That is [content strategy](../03-brand-story-and-content/content-strategy.md).
- The request is a brand-voice exercise with no commercial sentence.

## Words you will use

| Word | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Who it is for, what it is, why it is better, product type—one set, one primary ICP |
| **Hierarchy** | Core, then variants by seat / stage / channel. Variants do not rewrite the core |
| **Perception** | A storyline a buyer should be able to say about us. A message ladders to one |
| **Value line** | A distinct, durable benefit. Three is enough. Features sit under the line as proof |
| **Copy** | Words on one surface. Copy applies messaging; it is not a second positioning workshop |

## One rule

**Positioning → messaging → copy, in that order.** If the copy cannot still name the alternative, you skipped a layer. If every surface needs a new workshop, you never locked the core.

## Operating method

### Step 1: lock the core from positioning, not from a brainstorm

Write four lines that [homepage](../07-website-and-conversion/homepage.md) and [positioning](positioning.md) already require:

- who it is for (the primary ICP, not “GTM teams”);
- what it is (product type a buyer would search);
- why it is better than the **named** alternative;
- the door (signup, scoped conversation, or a named page).

Pick **one** product type so the comparator is not a committee:

| Product type | You are usually compared to |
|---|---|
| Vertical solution | The generic horizontal tool in a specific job |
| 10× better in a known category | A named incumbent |
| Buy vs build | The internal process or spreadsheet |
| New way | Doing nothing / the old way—not the other new-way startup |

“New way” is the hardest. Do not invent a category name to finish the sentence. Describe the job. Early lookalikes in an emerging space usually help you teach the problem; they are not the homepage enemy.

If you cannot pick a type, the audience is too wide or the product is not differentiated. Narrow the [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md). Do not mash types into one hero.

### Step 2: name the three value lines, each with proof

Under the core, write **three** value lines. Each must be distinct, last at least two quarters, and fail a paste test: if a competitor could put the same three lines on their site, they are not yours.

Under each line, list proof you actually have: a dated result, a named constraint you will not hide, a feature that causes the benefit. Unsourced superlatives go in the bin. A deeper promise-and-credibility worksheet stays planned as `value-proposition.md`; this step only keeps the stool standing.

### Step 3: ladder every message to a perception

[Content strategy](../03-brand-story-and-content/content-strategy.md) already wants three or four perceptions. Messaging does not invent a fifth. Write which perception each value line and each surface moves. If a request cannot name one, it is a random act of marketing. SEO and sales-enablement exceptions are written, not assumed.

### Step 4: branch the hierarchy; do not rewrite the core

Variants are allowed. A second story is not.

| Axis | What may change | What may not |
|---|---|---|
| ICP / seat | Emphasis, objection, proof, next step | Product type and the primary alternative |
| Funnel stage | Problem-aware vs product-aware language | The transformation you claim |
| Channel | Length, format, CTA | The perception |

The homepage hero speaks to the **primary** ICP. Other seats get their own URL, a [GACCS](../../templates/campaign-brief.md), or a talk track—not a second headline in the same hero. Scaling and testing ICPs do not get equal space; see [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md) maturity.

### Step 5: ship copy only from a filled row

One row per surface (homepage hero, outbound first paragraph, ads, first meeting). If the row cannot be completed, do not write. Then run the [homepage](../07-website-and-conversion/homepage.md) scan, the [pitch](sales-enablement.md), and one outbound paragraph against the same table. Contradiction is a defect.

## Teaching fill (invented—not a customer)

Ops-lead queue. Alternative: shared inbox + spreadsheet. Type: buy vs build.

| Field | Fill |
|---|---|
| Core | Queue software for customer-ops teams that still live in email. Better because the team can run SLAs without an ITSM rollout. Door: walk through *your* queue. |
| Perception | “A shared inbox breaks when someone is paid on queue health.” |
| Value lines | (1) The lead can see the queue without a war-room spreadsheet. (2) Security can find where the data lives. (3) Sales can forward one comparison URL. |
| Will not say | “AI-powered GTM platform.” “For every revenue team.” |
| Variant | Security seat: constraint page, not a second hero. Testing ICP: no homepage slot this quarter. |

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] [Positioning](positioning.md) worksheet is filled; this page does not invent a second market decision.
- [ ] One product type and one primary comparator are written.
- [ ] Three value lines are distinct and fail a competitor paste test.
- [ ] Each line has proof we can date or show, or we cut the claim.
- [ ] Each surface ladders to a named perception.
- [ ] Homepage hero is one primary ICP. Other seats have a URL or a written “not this quarter.”
- [ ] Homepage, first outbound paragraph, and demo opener do not contradict.
- [ ] A [GACCS](../../templates/campaign-brief.md) exists before a campaign writes new copy.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Surface agreement | Strangers or new hires name the same category, seat, and alternative from hero, outbound, and opener |
| Sales rewrite rate | Times an AE replaces the core in the first five minutes |
| Perception coverage | Non-SEO assets this month that cannot name a perception |
| Wrong-ICP traffic | Homepage conversations from seats we will not sell |

Do not count slogan variants, workshop decks, or “we shipped a messaging framework” as outcomes.

## Common mistakes

- Writing copy in the positioning workshop.
- Putting every ICP in the hero so nobody feels spoken to.
- Positioning a “new way” against the other new-way vendor instead of the old way.
- Three value lines that are the same benefit restated.
- Features with no transformation.
- Proof-free superlatives.
- A messaging doc sales will not paste into a thread.
- Letting brand personality substitute for a commercial sentence.

## Sources and evidence boundary

This is an owner-maintained operating synthesis. Claims on any live surface must stay inside dated product, customer, security, and performance evidence.

The split of product-marketing research vs positioning vs messaging vs copy, the instruction to pick one product type and comparator before writing, “new way” vs the old way rather than vs emerging lookalikes, and “refine for audience / stage / channel after the core” draw on Emily Kramer ([MKT1, 2024-06-12](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/positioning-guide?ref=b2b-playbook) and [2024-06-27](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/positioning-and-messaging-teardown?ref=b2b-playbook)). Named teardowns in those essays are **their** grades on **those** homepages on those dates—not a command to copy a layout. The paid statement templates are not reproduced here.

Homepage one-primary-ICP, transformation over feature lists, three distinct value lines with proof, and consistency across surfaces draw on Allyson Letteri’s public guest essay ([MKT1, 2024-05-20](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/messaging-mistakes-allyson-letteri?ref=b2b-playbook)). The 3Ps, 12 Jung archetypes, and USP formula in that essay are **not** this library’s operating system. Dunford’s 10-second scan and so-what ladder on [positioning](positioning.md) remain the market decision this page applies.

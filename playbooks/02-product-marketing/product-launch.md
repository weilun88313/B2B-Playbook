**Last reviewed:** 2026-09-03

A ship date is not a market event. A launch is a **campaign**: fuel plus engine for a named audience, nested under a perception, sized to a tier, with momentum after day one. The changelog is not the marketing calendar.

[Product Hunt](../04-channels-and-distribution/product-hunt.md) is a one-day amplifier. [Company cadence](../09-operations-pipeline-and-measurement/company-cadence.md) keeps launch week off the close. This page is whether the change deserves a coordinated event at all—and how PMM owns it after the announcement.

This is not a press-release checklist, not a feature factory, and not a reason to launch every ticket product ships.

## Use this when

- Product, marketing, and sales cannot say the tier, the audience, or what success is.
- Every release gets a blog post, a landing page, and an email because “that is the launch playbook.”
- The story is written in a vacuum, then handed to content the week of ship.
- Leadership expects journalists or Product Hunt to create the market.

## Do not use this when

- Positioning is empty. Finish [positioning](positioning.md) and [messaging](messaging.md) first.
- The change is a T3 content update. Fold it into existing pages and enablement; do not book a war room.
- You need a one-day hunter thread. That is [Product Hunt](../04-channels-and-distribution/product-hunt.md).
- Launch week sits on quarter-end. Move the date: [company cadence](../09-operations-pipeline-and-measurement/company-cadence.md).
- You are still hunting the first ten logos. Stay in [first ten](../01-strategy-and-buyers/first-ten-customers.md).

## Words you will use

| Word | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **Campaign** | Fuel + engine + audience + time box. Launch is one type, next to demand, brand, ABM, events |
| **Tier** | How much effort this change earns—not how proud the PM is |
| **Perception** | A storyline the launch must nest under. The feature is not a fifth story |
| **Launch positioning** | Who it is for, what it is, why better than the **named** alternative—for this change |
| **Day one** | The announcement. The job continues at 30 / 60 / 90 |

## One rule

**Audience calendar first, then slot launches that earn a campaign.** If this release is weaker than other campaigns you could run, bundle it, shrink it, or skip the announcement. A checklist is for gaps after strategy—not instead of it.

## Operating method

### Step 1: decide if it is launch-worthy; then pick a tier

Ask: is this more important than the other campaigns on the calendar—not “is it newer than last sprint?” Bundle features when the **story** is the same. Ship date and launch date may differ.

| Tier | What it is | Default effort |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | Trajectory change. Rare (often 1–2 / year) | All-hands campaign; drumbeat for weeks or months |
| **T2** | Real splash for one segment or one funnel stage (often ~1 / quarter) | Focused campaign; not the whole company |
| **T3** | Product changed; market does not need an announcement | Web, enablement, support, changelog. No “we are excited to announce” |

Align the tier with product and sales **before** assets. Tears later are a tier that was never written.

### Step 2: write GACCS and launch positioning before production

Fill [campaign-brief.md](../../templates/campaign-brief.md) (Goals, Audience, Creative, Channels, Stakeholders). Then write **positioning for the thing shipping**—comparator, who, what, why better—not the ideal press headline. Nest the narrative under one of the year’s [perceptions](../03-brand-story-and-content/content-strategy.md). Pair the product with other fuel when it helps (a data cut, a customer story, a funding or event moment)—do not launch the feature in a vacuum.

Take items **off** the inherited checklist before you check anything off. Channels that used to “deliver the audience on a platter” are noisier; press will not save a T2 feature. [Distribution is planned in the brief](../03-brand-story-and-content/content-strategy.md), not after publish.

### Step 3: align internally; use the ecosystem, not a press prayer

Marketing, product, and sales agree on the brief, the tier, and the success metric (adoption, velocity, expansion, a new ICP—pick one). Validate the job with customers **before** launch day. Give customers, partners, creators, and community a look **before** the public date; canned “excited to partner” quotes are not a substitute.

Journalists covering a feature launch are an exception for some T1s bundled with something larger. They are not the plan.

### Step 4: own momentum after day one

Product will move on. PMM (or whoever owns the story) does not. Write 30 / 60 / 90: follow-up content, enablement, a customer proof, a webinar, a lifecycle mention. Measure **use and why they are not using**, not only announcement traffic. [Company cadence](../09-operations-pipeline-and-measurement/company-cadence.md) still forbids stacking this on the close.

| GACCS | Goal: perception + adoption on existing accounts. Audience: core ICP. Channels: customer webinar, enablement, lifecycle—not a press embargo |
| Will not do | Product Hunt. “We’re excited to announce.” Launch blog as the strategy |
| After day one | Day 30: two customer clips. Day 60: comparison URL update. Day 90: expansion talk track |

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] Compared to other **campaigns**, not only to other releases.
- [ ] Tier is written; T3 is allowed.
- [ ] GACCS exists before copy or design.
- [ ] Launch positioning names the alternative.
- [ ] Narrative ladders to a locked perception.
- [ ] Product, marketing, and sales share the success metric.
- [ ] Distribution is in the brief, not a post-publish scramble.
- [ ] Day one is not the end of the plan.
- [ ] Date is not on the close week.
- [ ] Product Hunt is a choice, not the default.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Tier honesty | T1 count per year (should be rare) |
| Adoption / use | Whether the change is lived, not announced |
| Perception fit | Assets that cannot name the parent storyline |
| After-day-one | Work dated 30 / 60 / 90 vs a dead microsite |

Do not count blog publishes, embargo hits, or upvotes as the launch.

## Common mistakes

- Product roadmap = marketing roadmap.
- Launch story in a vacuum (no perception, no other fuel).
- Press or Product Hunt as the strategy.
- Checking every box on last year’s list.
- Internal misalignment until the week of ship.
- “We’re excited to announce…” as the opening.
- Measuring only top-of-funnel on launch week.
- Selling far ahead of a date the product cannot hit.

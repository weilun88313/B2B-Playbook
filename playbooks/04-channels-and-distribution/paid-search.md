Paid search captures an expressed query. The practical task is to connect that query to a useful answer and then check whether the resulting buyer progresses. A cheap click from the wrong question is still waste.

## When to use it

Start when customers can describe the problem in searchable language, an appropriate landing page exists, and sales or self-service can handle the response. For a new category with little explicit demand, validate problem queries before assuming category keywords will supply volume.

## Build the first campaign

1. **Group by buyer question.** Separate brand navigation, category evaluation, alternatives, and educational questions. Give each a budget and destination consistent with its purpose; do not let inexpensive branded demand conceal weak prospect acquisition.
2. **Write a query hypothesis.** Record likely intent, excluded meanings, and what evidence would invalidate the group. Review current match behavior in platform documentation. Exact match is not a guarantee of literal word-for-word matching.
3. **Connect the promise.** The ad and page should answer the same question, show relevant proof, and explain the next action. Avoid sending an integration query to a generic homepage.
4. **Define measurement before bidding.** Test conversion events, deduplication, attribution windows, and downstream qualification. A form submission and an accepted opportunity are different outcomes. Choose a bidding approach compatible with the available, reliable conversion signal; do not train toward a noisy event merely because it is frequent.
5. **Launch with a bounded loss.** Name the spend ceiling, review date, and owner. Inspect actual search terms where available and add negative keywords carefully. Overbroad exclusions can remove legitimate demand.
6. **Review economics after the sales lag.** Reconcile spend, valid inquiries, qualified opportunities, and wins by cohort. Keep pending outcomes visible. Change one major variable at a time so the next review is interpretable.

## Worked example

A hypothetical invoice platform buys traffic for “invoice approval software.” Search-term review finds people seeking a printable approval stamp. Those clicks are excluded, while the landing page is revised to show a multi-person approval flow and supported accounting systems.

The trial spends &#36;1,200 and receives 12 valid inquiries, of which three become qualified opportunities. That is &#36;100 per valid inquiry and &#36;400 per opportunity; it is not a customer acquisition cost because none has closed yet. The team waits for the cohort's outcome before claiming profitable acquisition.

## Before expanding

Verify tracking with a test inquiry, check landing-page behavior on a phone, and read a sample of actual inquiries with sales. Expand only where the intent, experience, and economics support it. Platform-reported conversions alone cannot establish incremental revenue.

title: "Content repurposing"
---

**Published:** 2026-09-12 · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

Repurposing preserves a useful idea while changing how a reader encounters and uses it. The aim is not to turn every article into every possible format. Choose a new format only when it improves access, understanding, or application.

## Establish the source of truth

Start from a strong, current asset with clear rights and supporting evidence. Identify its central argument, example, limitations, and intended action. If those are unclear, edit the original before multiplying it.

## Adapt without distorting

1. **Extract the essential idea.** Write one sentence explaining the claim and another explaining its conditions. Keep those together throughout adaptation.
2. **Choose a new reader task.** A checklist helps execution; a short video demonstrates movement; a diagram explains relationships; a social post opens a discussion. Format follows the task.
3. **Rebuild the structure.** Write for the destination rather than cropping arbitrary passages. Give a short piece enough context to stand alone and an appropriate link to the complete explanation.
4. **Preserve evidence and rights.** Carry necessary qualifications, attribution, and customer permissions into the new piece. Approval for a private presentation does not automatically cover a public advertisement.
5. **Review native usability.** Check captions, text size, alternative text, reading order, and mobile rendering. A slide that was legible on a projector may be unreadable in a feed.
6. **Connect maintenance.** Record derivatives against their canonical source, with owners and update triggers. When a claim changes, find and revise the versions that still repeat it.

## Worked example

A fictional article explains why a routing system needs a fallback owner. It becomes a one-page checklist for administrators, a short screen recording showing a failed match, and a discussion post asking how teams detect unassigned inquiries.

The recording uses test data and includes the product version. The post retains the caveat that fallback ownership must respect territory and availability rules. None of the derivatives claims that adding a fallback alone guarantees faster revenue.

## Review value rather than output count

Check whether the derivative helped its intended task: checklist use, successful setup, meaningful replies, or qualified progression. Ten weak fragments are not an improvement over one clear guide. Retire redundant versions when maintaining them becomes more costly than the access they provide.

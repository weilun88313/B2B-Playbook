An asset needs a route to the people it was created to help. Distribution planning specifies where a reader will encounter it, why it belongs there, and who will maintain that route after publication.

## Start with the reader's task

Choose one important asset and the moment it serves. An implementation checklist belongs in documentation, onboarding, and relevant support replies; a strategic point of view may belong in a newsletter or practitioner discussion. Availability in a resource center is only one route.

## Build the distribution plan

1. **Name the audience and moment.** Write the task the asset helps complete and what the reader should be able to do afterward.
2. **Inventory credible access.** List owned channels, permissioned customer communications, partner routes, and relevant third-party communities. Record audience overlap and participation rules.
3. **Adapt the introduction.** Explain the asset's value in each channel's own format. In a discussion, answer the question before offering a relevant link; obtain moderator permission where required.
4. **Assign the delivery.** Every placement needs an owner, date, canonical URL, and reason for being there. Give customer-facing teams a short usage note rather than an unexplained file attachment.
5. **Measure the useful progression.** Use consistent campaign parameters where appropriate, but retain an unmodified canonical destination. Separate reading, completion, and commercial follow-up; some valuable distribution has no measurable click.
6. **Maintain the route.** Review broken links, obsolete partner copies, and recurring placements. Update the existing destination when possible so older recommendations remain useful.

## Worked example

A hypothetical software team publishes a data-migration checklist. Marketing sends a short explanation to newsletter subscribers, implementation staff add it to their kickoff message, and a partner includes it in a permitted technical workshop.

The newsletter produces most visits, while kickoff use produces fewer repeated setup questions. The team keeps both routes because they serve different tasks. It does not judge the implementation placement by new leads, nor count every partner attendee as a new contact with marketing permission.

## Choose the next improvement

Read replies and ask the people delivering the asset whether it answered the actual question. Improve the destination if visitors arrive but cannot use it. Improve the placement if the right readers never encounter it. More channels are not automatically more effective: remove routes that require repeated effort without a credible audience or outcome.

**Published:** 2026-09-12 · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

Customer advocacy helps willing customers share an honest experience in a format they choose. It should protect their time, approval requirements, and relationship with your company. A satisfied account is not automatically an available reference.

## Match the request to the person

Distinguish a private reference call, public quote, case study, event appearance, and peer introduction. Each requires its own scope of permission and effort. Start by asking what participation, if any, the customer would welcome.

## Run a respectful program

1. **Record willingness and scope.** Track approved topics, formats, regions, review requirements, and availability. Include an easy way to pause or decline.
2. **Match relevance.** Choose advocates whose actual experience addresses the prospective buyer's question. Do not imply they used features or achieved results outside their experience.
3. **Prepare a precise brief.** Explain the audience, purpose, expected time, questions, publication channels, and any compensation or benefit. Obtain necessary personal and organizational approval.
4. **Preserve the customer's voice.** Fact-check names and figures, distinguish edits from rewritten claims, and give the agreed review opportunity. Never invent or materially alter a quote to strengthen the pitch.
5. **Track requests and follow through.** Coordinate across sales, marketing, and customer success so the same person is not repeatedly approached. Thank them and share the finished material or outcome as agreed.
6. **Review continued suitability.** Refresh permissions and claims when the relationship or product changes. Pause requests during unresolved service problems rather than treating advocacy as an entitlement.

## Worked example

A fictional customer agrees to one private reference call about implementation. A salesperson later wants to use a sentence from the call on the website. The program owner requests separate publication approval instead of assuming the original agreement covers a public testimonial.

The customer declines publicity but remains willing to take an occasional relevant call. The system records that preference, preserving a useful relationship without turning every advocate into a public spokesperson.

## Evaluate sustainably

Track fulfilled relevant requests, customer feedback, and contribution to buyer understanding. Do not rank advocates only by revenue influenced. Inspect concentration: if a few customers carry most requests, expand the pool carefully or reduce demand.

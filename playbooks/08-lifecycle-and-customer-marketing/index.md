> **Decision owned:** How should the company educate, retain, expand, and mobilize customers after initial interest or purchase?

**Status:** Domain guide published · 9 tactic playbooks published

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-30

## Scope

This domain coordinates communication and value delivery across the relationship after a buyer first identifies themselves and especially after purchase. It connects nurture, onboarding communication, education, adoption, community, advocacy, expansion, and renewal.

It owns audience-state communication, customer education, lifecycle programs, community participation, review and referral programs, advocacy, and expansion or renewal support. It does not own the product's in-app user experience or a full ITIL support stack.

**Customer success** operating cadence, **onboarding** (work + messages), the **CS executive’s first 90 days**, and the **post-sale record** sit here as GTM lifecycle operations. Ticket tooling remains support.

## Core decisions

1. Which lifecycle states require different information, proof, or action?
2. What should a person understand and achieve before moving to the next state?
3. Which behavior or signal should trigger communication?
4. How should marketing, sales, success, support, and product share ownership?
5. What value does the customer receive before being asked for advocacy, referral, or expansion?
6. Which messages should be automated, and which require human judgment?
7. How will engagement, adoption, retention, expansion, and customer trust be measured?
8. Who owns renewals and upsells, and what does a week of CS actually inspect?
9. What is education versus implementation, and how do we measure launch versus value?
10. What must a new customer receive in writing before and after kickoff?
11. What should a new CS executive actually do in 90 days?
12. Which post-sale fields must exist so CS can see value, cadence, renewal, and risk?
13. How do we measure revenue leaving the book without letting net hide the leak?

## Recommended build order

1. Define lifecycle states, ownership, entry criteria, and success outcomes.
2. If customers already exist, write [customer success](customer-success.md) (who owns the book) and [customer onboarding](customer-onboarding.md) (how they reach first value), then [onboarding communication](onboarding-communication.md) (what they receive in writing).
3. A new CS executive uses [CS-leadership ramp](cs-leadership-ramp.md). The record they audit is [CS workspace](cs-workspace.md). Gross vs net on that book is [revenue churn](revenue-churn.md).
4. Known people who are not ready for a walk use [lead nurture](lead-nurture.md) by state—not a seven-email drip. Customer education stays planned.
5. Connect product, success, support, and CRM signals to lifecycle programs.
6. Add community, advocacy, review, and referral motions after customer value is proven.
7. Introduce additional value only on evidence ([expansion marketing](expansion-marketing.md)). Recognize value before the commercial path ([renewal marketing](renewal-marketing.md)). Win-back stays planned.

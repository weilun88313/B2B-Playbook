# Customer success

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-29

Customer success is not “be nice after the sale.” It is **people, process, and performance management** on the book you already won. If renewals, expansion, and support each have a different story of the customer, you do not have a CS motion. You have three teams sharing a logo.

This domain’s job after purchase is to educate, retain, expand, and mobilize. This page is the operating system: who owns the account, what a week looks like, how a ticket becomes a closed loop, how a renewal is tracked, and what a new CS hire must learn. It does not replace in-app UX or a full ITIL support stack. It does insist that marketing, sales, and success share **tiers, handoff facts, and a number**.

[Customer onboarding](customer-onboarding.md) is the first-value path (education vs implementation). Read that next if the wound is “they never launched.” This page is the ongoing machine.

## Use this when

- Someone just signed and sales is “throwing it over the wall.”
- CS, support, and account management cannot say who owns renewals and upsells.
- Churn is explained as a vibe. There is no book, no tier, no weekly at-risk list.
- You are hiring the first CSM and the only artifact is a culture slide.

## Do not use this when

- There are no customers yet. Stay in [first ten](../01-strategy-and-buyers/first-ten-customers.md).
- You need the first-meeting pitch. That is [sales enablement](../02-product-marketing/sales-enablement.md).
- You need employee SDR ramp. That is [SDR onboarding](../05-outbound-and-prospecting/sdr-onboarding.md).
- Legal/privacy owns the relationship (health, government). Get a qualified owner.

## One rule

**Write where renewals and upsells live, and how those people are paid, before you write values.** If CS is compensated on logo hugs and sales is compensated on expansion, you have designed a war. Point at [sales compensation](../09-operations-pipeline-and-measurement/sales-compensation.md) for the live plan.

## Operating method

### Step 1: put history on one page so the team is not guessing

A new CS hire should not learn the book from folklore. Write:

- Count of customers, revenue in the book, mix by industry / size / region if it changes how you staff
- Who spends, who is engaged, who is referenceable—named, not “we have logos”

This is context, not a vanity dashboard. If you cannot name referenceable customers, advocacy is not the next motion. Honesty is.

### Step 2: name the jobs, then the org chart

Roles only exist if the sentence “this person is paid to ______” is different.

Typical split (labels vary; the work does not):

- **Associate / CSA:** queue, first response, known how-tos. Time-bound (for example first response in hours you can actually hit).
- **CSM:** adoption, risk, QBR-shaped conversations, the relationship after launch.
- **Account manager:** commercial expansion if that is *not* the CSM—or write that the CSM owns both.
- **Senior AM / lead:** named top accounts, exceptions, hiring bar.

Draw the boxes only after the sentences exist. A four-title ladder with one person in each box is a costume.

### Step 3: say how CS is measured—team and individual

Team weekly: at-risk accounts, percent to the CS number, business reviews this week.

Individual 1:1: QBRs done, tickets received/answered if they own a queue, renewal win rate, churn. Break the scorecard by role if an associate should not be judged as a hunter.

If you need a town hall, its job is **wins and losses across tiers**, not a pep talk.

### Step 4: the sales → CS handoff is a conversation with a file

Close is not the handoff. The handoff is:

1. Agreement signed; the facts CS needs are in the system of record (not only in the AE’s head).
2. Sales introduces the people who will live through onboarding, invoicing, and day-to-day.
3. CS (or implementation—see [customer onboarding](customer-onboarding.md)) owns kickoff, the project tool, and the success metric the customer already heard.
4. The commercial owner (CSM or AM) stays in the relationship instead of vanishing until renewal.

Standardize the briefing: pain, promised solution, goals, dependencies, advocate vs detractor, how we will know they want to renew. Async video is allowed. A two-line CRM note is not.

### Step 5: support is a path, not a personality

Write where requests come from (inbox, in-app, bot, named queue). Then the path:

Request → automated ack → first human response → resolved **or** escalated to technical support → closed.

If CSMs are bad at implementation, do not hide implementation inside “be a CSM.” Split the work. Introduce support at the **start** of a project, not as a surprise at go-live.

### Step 6: renewals, upsells, advocacy are playbooks with triggers

Your CS file should answer:

- What happens at a **success milestone** that implies expansion—and who orchestrates it internally and with the customer
- What happens at a milestone that implies **advocacy** (public or internal)
- What happens when an **external trigger** (new exec, competitor, usage spike) exposes expansion or advocacy
- How renewals are **created and tracked** in the CRM; whether upsells are a different object; how churn is reported

Tiers (named 1/2/3 in CRM) exist to change coverage and QBR depth—not to print a slide. Coverage that cannot be staffed is a lie.

Write the **exception plays** before you need them (one page each, not a novel): champion leaves (continuity in the account **and** whether they are a new-logo motion elsewhere—do not mix the two); customer goes dark; invoice unpaid; expected churn vs surprise churn; they outgrow the product. The CS file that only describes the happy path is a brochure.

### Step 7: ramp CS like a job, not a shadow week

A new hire needs, in writing: product, this playbook, shadowing, demo/call listening, email/call patterns, renewal conversation, CRM how-to, territories and rules of engagement, [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md). Then live queue or a tiny book with a manager on the first accounts. Same spirit as [SDR onboarding](../05-outbound-and-prospecting/sdr-onboarding.md): **exit tests**, not “sit with Jordan.”

Values (customer-first, speed, teamwork) go last. They cannot substitute for a compensation program or a staffing plan.

## Teaching fill (invented—not a customer)

Forty customers. One CSM, one associate. AE still closes. Renewals sit in CS; upsells over a threshold return to the AE with CS attached.

| Field | Fill |

| Book | 40 logos · ~$2.1M ARR · two industries dominate · five accounts are 40% of revenue |
| CSA job | First response < 4 business hours on the shared queue; known articles; escalate config |
| CSM job | Adoption on all; QBR on the five; at-risk list every Monday |
| Handoff | AE books 30 minutes; CRM fields: advocate, detractor, promised outcome, renew-test |
| Support path | Email + in-app → ack → CSA → CSM if commercial · engineer if product defect |
| Renewal | Opportunity created 120 days out; stage names match [forecasting](../09-operations-pipeline-and-measurement/forecasting.md) |
| Milestone | “First dashboard used by finance” → expansion conversation owned by CSM, AE on the call |
| Weekly | At-risk (red/yellow), renewals this quarter, tickets aging > 48h. No leaderboard. |

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] Renewals and upsells have a home and a pay rule.
- [ ] Each CS role has a one-sentence job.
- [ ] Handoff is a meeting plus fields, not a Slack “fyi.”
- [ ] Ticket path has a clock and an escalation.
- [ ] Tiers change work, not only a label.
- [ ] At-risk is a weekly list with owners.
- [ ] New hires certify on ICP, CRM, and handoff—not only product tours.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Renewal win rate and logo vs revenue churn | Whether the book is healthy |
| Time-to-handoff after close | Wall-throwing |
| First-response and reopen rate | Support path vs theater |
| AE-accept of CS expansion intros | Whether commercial ownership is shared |
| At-risk list age | Whether red accounts are worked |

Do not count town halls held, values posters, or resemblance to a sample 2021 CS deck as success.

## Common mistakes

- CS compensated on “happiness,” sales on expansion, nobody on retention.
- Four titles, one human.
- Handoff as a converted opportunity with no briefing.
- Support introduced at go-live.
- Tiers in a slide, equal coverage in the calendar.
- QBRs with no at-risk list.
- Copying another company’s CS org because the titles looked mature.

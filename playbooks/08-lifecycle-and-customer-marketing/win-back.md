title: "Win-back"

**Published:** 2026-09-12 · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

Win-back reopens a conversation with an inactive or former customer when there is a credible reason the product may now fit. Start with why the relationship stopped. A discount does not resolve every reason for leaving.

## Separate the situations

Distinguish a canceled customer, an inactive user in a healthy account, a failed payment, and a stalled evaluation. They have different owners and needs. Verify contact eligibility and suppression before planning promotional re-engagement.

## Build a reason to return

1. **Recover the original reason.** Use cancellation notes, support history, and direct feedback where appropriately available. Mark unknown reasons as unknown rather than guessing from low usage.
2. **Identify what changed.** A missing integration may now exist; an implementation problem may have been fixed; the customer's needs may have changed. Verify the change before making the claim.
3. **Select an eligible cohort.** Include only accounts for which the change is relevant and contact is appropriate. Exclude unresolved issues that make a return premature.
4. **Write a specific invitation.** Acknowledge the prior context without overfamiliarity. Explain the change, its limits, and an optional low-friction way to reassess. Do not imply urgency that does not exist.
5. **Plan reactivation.** Check data retention, account restoration, current pricing, migration, and support. Do not promise that old data or settings remain available without verification.
6. **Measure durable return.** Separate replies, restarted trials, paid reactivation, and continued useful adoption. Compare servicing and incentive costs with retained value over an appropriate window.

## Worked example

A fictional customer canceled because a required accounting connector was unsupported. The connector is now available for the relevant version. The vendor verifies that scope and sends an eligible, concise invitation to review the new compatibility guide.

The message does not promise automatic restoration of the old workspace. If the customer returns, onboarding checks current requirements and data availability. Success is sustained useful use, not merely accepting a discounted first month.

## Know when to stop

Respect refusals and suppression. If the original mismatch remains, record it for product or market learning rather than repeatedly changing the subject line. A clean exit can preserve trust and future relevance better than a sequence with no new reason to engage.

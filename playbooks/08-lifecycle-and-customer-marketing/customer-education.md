Customer education helps people perform a task successfully with the product. The curriculum should follow the user's work and role, not simply the order of features in the interface.

## Choose an observable learning outcome

Begin with a task that matters to adoption: configure a workflow, interpret a result, troubleshoot a failure, or administer access. State what the learner should be able to do independently afterward.

## Build the learning path

1. **Identify prerequisites and roles.** Separate administrators, daily users, and decision-makers where their tasks differ. Check access, prior knowledge, and available practice environments.
2. **Choose the smallest useful lesson.** Teach one coherent task with enough context to understand why it matters. Link to reference material instead of reading every setting aloud.
3. **Demonstrate and let the learner practice.** Use realistic test data, show the expected result, and include a recoverable mistake. A video watched to completion does not prove the learner can perform the task.
4. **Provide feedback and help.** Explain common errors and how to get assistance. Make recordings, captions, transcripts, or written alternatives available as appropriate.
5. **Assess the actual capability.** Use a completed task, reviewed configuration, or short scenario. Avoid badges that imply expertise beyond what was assessed.
6. **Connect maintenance to product changes.** Assign an owner and version applicability. Retire or update lessons when the interface, workflow, or recommended practice changes.

## Worked example

A fictional workflow product teaches administrators to create an approval rule. The lesson includes a test request that intentionally has no matching owner. Learners must configure a fallback and explain why the exception occurred.

This reveals whether they understand the operating model, not just whether they can copy a screen. Support reviews recurring mistakes and updates the lesson where the explanation is weak. If many learners fail the same step, the product team also investigates the interface.

## Evaluate the program

Compare successful task performance, time to independent use, and relevant support patterns. Account for differences in customer complexity before attributing adoption improvements to training. Course enrollment is a reach measure; learning and customer outcomes need their own evidence.

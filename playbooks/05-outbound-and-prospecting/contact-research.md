Contact research identifies an appropriate person and a permitted route for a business conversation. Finding an address is only one part of the work. You also need a reason that this person owns or understands the problem.

## Start from the account question

Use this workflow after account research has established a plausible business need. Avoid collecting people simply because a database makes them available. A role hypothesis can be wrong even when the address is deliverable.

## Build a minimal contact brief

1. **Identify the responsibility.** Name the workflow, decision, or implementation task you need to discuss. Translate that responsibility into plausible roles without assuming titles are consistent across companies.
2. **Check current professional evidence.** Use company pages, published professional profiles, event biographies, or an approved data provider. Record source and date. Distinguish a confirmed responsibility from an inference.
3. **Choose the least intrusive suitable route.** Prefer the person's published business route or another route approved for the intended use. Do not enrich with private life details or sensitive traits just to manufacture personalization.
4. **Review eligibility.** Check jurisdiction, channel rules, provenance, applicable notices or permissions, and internal suppression. A publicly visible address or provider contract does not by itself establish permission for every use.
5. **Resolve ambiguity.** If two people plausibly own the task, write a neutral routing question or use an appropriate company contact route. Do not pretend to have inside knowledge.
6. **Set a freshness rule.** Record a review date and remove or correct stale role information. Keep only fields needed for the stated purpose and retention policy.

## Worked example

A hypothetical analytics vendor wants to discuss how a retailer reconciles weekly figures. A public job description suggests finance operations owns the process; a profile says someone manages reporting, but does not confirm procurement authority.

The brief marks that person as a possible workflow owner, not an economic buyer. The message asks whether reconciliation sits with their team and offers a relevant example. If they redirect the sender, the new contact is assessed on its own merits; an introduction does not erase channel or suppression requirements.

## Quality check

A second person should be able to explain why this contact is relevant without reading a flattering personalized sentence. Sample briefs for stale roles, unsupported authority claims, and missing provenance. Measure useful routing and conversations, not merely the number of records enriched.

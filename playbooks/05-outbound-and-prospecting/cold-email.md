# Cold email

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-24

Cold email tests whether a relevant account and buying seat will engage with a specific business hypothesis. Email is the delivery channel, not the strategy.

## Use this when

- An ICP and observable trigger already exist.
- [Account research](account-research.md) is complete.
- The objective is a qualified conversation or a clear no.
- The account list comes from explainable sources.

## Do not use this when

- The buying committee is reduced to “the decision-maker.”
- Open rate is treated as success.
- Compliance for the target country and channel has not been reviewed.
- Sending volume is compensating for weak account evidence.

## Operating method

### Step 1: define the smallest viable segment

Group accounts by a shared buyer, business job, current alternative, and observable trigger. Industry and job title alone rarely create a useful segment.

Begin with a small batch. The purpose is to learn whether the evidence and message produce qualified conversations, not to maximize activity.

### Step 2: confirm the research brief

Use the [account-research playbook](account-research.md). Confirm account fit, trigger, current alternative, buying seat, sources, unknowns, and a stop condition before writing.

### Step 3: write a two-sentence first message

Sentence one connects observable evidence to a business consequence. Sentence two asks one low-friction question or proposes one decision-specific next step.

> [Observed trigger] suggests [business job or risk] may be changing at [account]. Teams in that situation often find [specific limitation of the current alternative]; is that already solved, or worth comparing for [bounded next step]?

Avoid invented familiarity, unsupported performance claims, feature dumps, and calendar links without context.

### Step 4: decide whether email is enough

Use a short email-only path when written evidence and a reply are sufficient. When phone, LinkedIn, partners, events, or direct mail each have a distinct job, use the [multichannel-sequence playbook](multichannel-sequence.md). Never repeat the same message across channels.

### Step 5: classify replies by evidence

| Reply | Meaning | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Confirms problem and timing | Qualified signal | Resolve committee and schedule a working conversation |
| Corrects the premise | Valuable learning | Update account and segment evidence |
| Refers another person | Committee signal | Contact the referred seat with context |
| Not now with a date or event | Timing signal | Record the trigger and pause |
| No relevance / remove | Disqualifier or consent signal | Stop and update records |

### Step 6: review the segment, not only the copy

Weak replies can come from the wrong accounts, wrong seat, weak trigger, poor timing, low trust, deliverability, or copy. Do not rewrite the message before checking the other variables.

## Copyable templates

### First message

**Subject:** [trigger or decision]

> [Evidence connected to the account]. [Specific consequence or alternative limitation]. Is that already solved, or worth [bounded next step]?

### Close-the-loop message

> I have not found enough evidence that [hypothesis] is a current priority, so I will close the loop here. If [dated trigger or operating condition] changes, the useful comparison would be [specific artifact or next step].

## Pre-send checklist

- [ ] The account passes ICP disqualifiers.
- [ ] A dated or observable trigger exists.
- [ ] The target seat has a reason to care.
- [ ] Facts and assumptions are distinguishable.
- [ ] The message names a business consequence, not a feature list.
- [ ] The next step is bounded and relevant.
- [ ] The sequence adds a new angle at each step.
- [ ] Stop conditions and suppression handling are configured.
- [ ] Links, domains, mailboxes, and reply routing were tested.

## Compliance minimum

- [ ] A qualified owner reviewed the target jurisdictions and channels.
- [ ] The lawful basis, notices, sender identification, and opt-out handling are documented where required.
- [ ] Suppression requests propagate across tools.
- [ ] Data sources and permitted uses are recorded.
- [ ] Sensitive, private, or scraped data is not used merely because it is accessible.
- [ ] Platform terms and sending limits are reviewed.

This checklist is not legal advice.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Qualified positive reply rate | Measures conversations that confirm problem, timing, or next action—not polite interest |
| Premise correction rate | Reveals whether account research is learning anything |
| Meeting-to-qualified-opportunity rate | Tests whether replies come from the right accounts and seats |
| Disqualification rate | Shows whether the segment has real boundaries |
| Committee progression | Tracks movement from one contact to the required buying seats |
| Stop-rule compliance | Prevents stale accounts and consent failures from recycling |

Open rate is a deliverability diagnostic, not a business outcome. Sent volume is not progress.

## Common mistakes

- Personalizing around irrelevant personal detail.
- Treating a generic company fact as a buying trigger.
- Asking for a meeting before giving the recipient a reason to care.
- Hiding uncertainty inside confident claims.
- Continuing after a correction, opt-out, or disqualification.

## What to read next

One good email is not a system. Put it in a [multichannel sequence](multichannel-sequence.md). The meeting that email books should use the [pitch](../02-product-marketing/sales-enablement.md), not a feature tour.

## Sources and evidence boundary

This is an owner-maintained operating synthesis. Sender identity, data use, notices, opt-out handling, and other requirements vary by jurisdiction and channel; review them with qualified owners. This playbook is not legal advice.

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-29

A login, a Slack intro, and “shadow someone for a week” is not onboarding. It is how a new SDR invents a private version of the job: their own definition of a meeting, their own CRM hygiene, their own idea of what “qualified” means. Ninety days later you have activity and no system.

SDR and BDR titles fight in every company. On this page they mean the same job family: **people paid to create a qualified conversation**, not to close the contract. If they carry a close quota, they are not in this chapter—use [sales compensation](../09-operations-pipeline-and-measurement/sales-compensation.md) and [sales enablement](../02-product-marketing/sales-enablement.md).

The product of the role is not a dial. It is a meeting the account executive can run without reconstructing the story from a two-line CRM note. Train in that order: **who the job is → what the number is → how the system of record works → how you create the meeting → how we look at the work**. Skip to sequences on day two and you will spend month two unteaching bad CRM.

This page is not employment, wage-hour, or discrimination advice. Ramp, PIP language, and commission documents need qualified counsel where you hire.

## Use this when

- You are hiring the first SDR/BDR, or the second one who will not have the founder sitting next to them.
- New hires “know Salesforce” and still cannot say who owns a lead, what a good meeting is, or how they get paid.
- AEs complain that meetings are junk and SDRs complain that AEs ghost the handoff—and nobody wrote either rule.
- Outbound is the [primary motion](../04-channels-and-distribution/channel-strategy.md) and the only training is a sequence template.

## Do not use this when

- The founder still hunts the [first ten](../01-strategy-and-buyers/first-ten-customers.md) by hand. A curriculum for a team of zero is a stall. Write [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md) and do the work.
- You do not yet know whether outbound should exist. That is [channel strategy](../04-channels-and-distribution/channel-strategy.md).
- You need the mechanics of one email or one sequence. Those are already written: [cold email](cold-email.md), [multichannel sequence](multichannel-sequence.md). Come back here to put a *person* through them.
- You need the full legal employment file. This page will not write your handbook.

## One rule

**Do not train someone into a job you have not written.** If the manager cannot point to a one-page job, a credit rule, a CRM status list, and an AE handoff definition, the new hire will fill the blanks. Their blanks will not match yours.

## Operating method

Do these in order. The sequence is the method. Mixing “go book meetings” with “we will explain CRM later” is how garbage enters the calendar.

### Step 1: write the job before day one

Onboarding fails in the week *before* start, when nobody can answer the new hire’s first four questions.

Write, on one page:

- **Team structure.** Who manages them, how many people sit in the role, how territories or inbound queues are cut.
- **The job in one sentence.** Same test as compensation: *this person is paid to ______, measured as ______.*
- **What “good” looks like when nobody is watching.** Hours, coverage of the book, what they may invent vs what they must follow.
- **Promotion path.** To senior SDR, to AE, to “this is the job.” If there is no path, say so. A fake AE promise is how you lose people at month six.

If you cannot finish the page, you are not ready to hire. You are still designing the motion.

### Step 2: put the number in writing—including the ugly parts

Activity without a number is theater. A number without a day-in-the-life is cruelty.

Train these as one block, not as HR trivia at the end:

- **A standard day.** What a competent person actually does between 9 and 6: research, sequence work, live conversations, CRM, AE follow-up. If the day cannot fit the activity target, the target is a wish.
- **Daily activity expectation.** Name the unit that is *diagnostic* (conversations, personalized sends) versus the unit that is *vanity* (dials). [Account research](account-research.md) still gates who they touch.
- **Quota.** Meetings that match the qualification bar—not “any calendar hold.” Write the bar here; do not let each AE invent one.
- **How they get paid.** Point at the live [compensation](../09-operations-pipeline-and-measurement/sales-compensation.md) plan: credit metric, when a meeting counts, accelerators, ramps. Do not teach a second oral version.
- **PIP.** A written policy with a clock and a save path, or you do not have a PIP—you have a mood. Teach it on day one so it is not a surprise weapon.

A hire who cannot explain quota and credit by the end of week one is not ramping. They are guessing.

### Step 3: CRM is the law, not a filing cabinet

If two SDRs would treat the same inbound lead differently, you do not have rules of engagement. You have folklore.

Teach until they can do it without a manager in the chair:

- **Rules of engagement.** Who may touch which account; what happens when two people collide; what “working” means in hours, not in feelings.
- **Assignment.** Round-robin inbound vs territory vs named accounts. Write the tie-break. “Whoever grabbed it” is not a rule.
- **Required fields at lead level.** The fields an AE needs to start. Empty title and “interested in demo” is not a handoff.
- **Lead (or contact) statuses.** Each status is a *state with a next action*, not a color. “Working” that lasts three weeks with no task is a leak.
- **The working set.** Which views they live in, which reports they own, which tasks the system must create, which reminders fire if a human forgets.

Do not graduate them to live sequences until they can change a status, log the next step, and explain *why* the record looks like that.

### Step 4: the meeting is the product—train the last mile first

Sequences are easy to romanticize. The last mile is where the job is won or lost.

Before they run a 12-step cadences, they must be able to:

1. **Ask for a specific time**, not “are you free sometime.”
2. **Send a calendar invite** the buyer can accept, with the AE (or the right host) on it if that is the rule.
3. **Confirm** the day before. A hold that nobody confirmed is not a meeting.
4. **Track no-shows** as a status and a recovery action, not as vanished pipeline.
5. **Convert / assign to the AE** in the CRM *and* send the briefing: who, why now, what was promised, what was *not* promised, disqualifiers already tested.
6. **Know what success looks like after the meeting**—showed, no-show, disqualified, opportunity—so they do not keep “credit” for a ghost.

The planned SDR-handoff page in this domain will go deeper on context. Until it exists, the briefing in step 5 *is* the handoff. A converted lead with no note is a meeting the AE will burn.

### Step 5: then teach how they create the conversation

Now the motion. Split inbound and outbound qualification; they are not the same questions.

- **Inbound.** Why they raised a hand, whether they match [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md), what to do if they do not.
- **Outbound.** Evidence first—[account research](account-research.md)—then a sequence. Do not let ramp-week volume substitute for a reason to write.
- **Sequence design.** Number of steps, idle time between steps, subject lines, how much is allowed to stay templated vs what *must* be custom. The working method lives in [multichannel sequence](multichannel-sequence.md) and [cold email](cold-email.md). Onboarding’s job is to make those pages *mandatory reading with a live example from your book*, not a second competing theory.
- **The sales-engagement tool.** How they see tomorrow’s steps, which reports they use, when they are allowed to change stage. A tool full of overdue steps is not “hustle.” It is a broken operating cadence.

If the sequence library is a junk drawer of 40 cadences, fix that before you train. Onboarding cannot rescue a messy system.

### Step 6: show them how the company will look at them

People optimize the meeting they know is coming.

- **Weekly operating review.** What is on the slide: pipeline of meetings, show rate, qualification yield, AE feedback—not a pride round of dial counts unless you have already admitted that dials are the diagnostic.
- **Their own dashboards.** “How do I see meetings I set?” and “How do I see activity?” should have one canonical answer each. If the answer is “export from three tools,” you will train Excel heroes, not SDRs.
- **Leaderboards, games, SPIFs.** Optional spice. If the leaderboard is the only scoreboard, you will get sandbagging and junk meetings. Put them after the real metrics, or skip them until the job is stable.

### Step 7: ramp is a calendar, not a vibe

Write the weeks. Example shape (invented, not a law): week 1 = job + number + CRM sandbox; week 2 = last-mile meeting + listen to live calls; week 3 = live inbound or a tiny outbound slice with a manager on the first ten; weeks 4–8 = full motion with quota ramped; certification = they can explain credit, statuses, and a handoff on a real record without notes.

Shadowing is a method *inside* a week, not a substitute for the curriculum. “Sit with Jordan” is not a syllabus unless Jordan is teaching from the same written job.

## Teaching fill (invented—not a customer)

Two SDRs. Inbound demo requests plus a small outbound slice on a named list. One AE. CRM is the system of record; a sales-engagement tool runs the sequences.

| Field | Fill |
|---|---|
| Job sentence | Paid to create **qualified first meetings** with ICP accounts; measured as meetings that show and that the AE accepts as in-profile. |
| Structure | One manager (the AE lead). No territories yet; inbound is round-robin. Outbound list is a shared named-account queue with a 48-hour lock. |
| Standard day | 90 minutes research / personalization · live queue 11:00–12:30 and 16:00–17:00 · CRM and AE briefs after every book · no “power hour” of unlogged dials. |
| Quota (month 3) | 8 qualified meetings set · show-rate target is a diagnostic, not extra quota. |
| Credit | Counts when the invite is accepted and the AE is on the hold. No-show: no credit; recovery sequence is required. AE reject-as-out-of-profile: no credit, written reason. |
| CRM statuses | New → Attempting → Connected → Meeting set → Showed / No-show / Disqualified / Handed to AE. “Working” is not a status. |
| Required fields before convert | Account, title, trigger or inbound source, ICP yes/no, promised agenda, disqualifiers already asked, next step owner. |
| Sequence | One inbound cadence, one outbound cadence. Max 8 steps. Custom first line required on outbound. See the live examples in [cold email](cold-email.md) / [sequence](multichannel-sequence.md). |
| Week 1 exit test | Explain assignment, statuses, quota, and credit on a whiteboard. |
| Week 3 exit test | Book one inbound meeting with a manager listening; send the AE brief the AE would actually use. |
| Weekly review | 25 minutes: meetings set, shows, AE accepts/rejects, three records with bad hygiene. No leaderboard until month two. |

Copy the empty fields. Do not copy the “8 meetings” as a benchmark.

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] The job, number, and CRM statuses exist as documents a hire can keep.
- [ ] Compensation credit for a meeting matches what onboarding teaches.
- [ ] Assignment and rules of engagement settle collisions without a Slack vote.
- [ ] “Qualified meeting” is the same sentence for SDR and AE.
- [ ] Last-mile (invite, confirm, no-show, AE brief) is trained before 12-step sequences.
- [ ] Inbound and outbound qualification are separate, and both sit on [ICP](../01-strategy-and-buyers/icp.md).
- [ ] Sequence library is small enough to teach; [cold email](cold-email.md) / [sequence](multichannel-sequence.md) are the method, not a rival deck.
- [ ] One dashboard answers “my meetings” and one answers “my activity.”
- [ ] Ramp weeks have exit tests, not only shadowing.
- [ ] Leaderboards and SPIFs cannot outrun show rate and AE accept rate.

## Metrics

| Metric | Diagnostic use |
|---|---|
| Time-to-first-certified-handoff | Whether the curriculum actually ends |
| AE accept rate of SDR meetings | Whether “qualified” is shared |
| Show rate | Last-mile (confirm, invite quality) vs junk booking |
| Status hygiene (records stuck in “working”) | CRM law vs folklore |
| Ramp-week quota vs live quota | Whether the day can hold the target |
| Unplanned mid-ramp process changes | Curriculum was incomplete |

Do not count completed slide decks, badges, or days sat next to a top performer as success.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with the sequence tool because it looks like work.
- Training activity targets that cannot fit in a real day.
- A second oral commission story that disagrees with the plan.
- Round-robin *and* territory *and* “whoever is hungry,” with no tie-break.
- Statuses that do not force a next task.
- Counting a calendar hold as quota when the AE was never briefed.
- Letting AEs reject meetings with no written reason—then blaming SDRs for “quality.”
- Forty cadences on day five.
- Leaderboard as the only scoreboard.
- PIP as a surprise in month two with no written policy on day one.
- Calling founder-led hunting “SDR onboarding” so you can delay [first ten](../01-strategy-and-buyers/first-ten-customers.md).
